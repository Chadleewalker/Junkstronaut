01 Top 5 Issues

1
Blocking Strengthened

The win object is mechanically unlandable — the satellite doubles ship mass while the fixed, non-upgradeable chute and one-way staging leave no lever to buy its touchdown speed under the fail line.

Chute descent speed grows with √mass and ships at fixed mid-tier values; the three purchasable upgrades never reduce it, and storage worsens it. "Landable only near full upgrades" describes a capability the upgrade tree does not contain. Two isolated reviewers reconstructed the same blocker from the same passages; the damaged-satellite ending becomes the mechanically default outcome, not a risk.

02 The Disagreement

Does "a lazy run still breaks even" describe a floor, a trap, or a reassurance?

Systems Designer · Business Analyst

Read as a floor: a guaranteed break-even removes downward pressure, producing a monotonic patience-grind (Systems). As a marketed safety net it removes early-game stakes and delays the anxiety pillar (Business). The reassurance reads as real to a prospective player.

Adversarial QA

Read as a trap: break-even holds only for a clean lazy run. Once a hard landing damages cargo and adds a repair bill, the floor is gone and the balance spirals below launch price into an undefined, unrecoverable bankruptcy state with no loss screen.

Escalated Cross-examination partly converged — Systems ceded the "sharper reading" to QA, Business withdrew "safety net" for "unverified reassurance." The design ruling underneath is unresolved: does break-even actually hold, and what is the intended economic fail/recovery state? The document quantifies no price, so it supports all three.

Is the game anxious, or a scrapyard comedy?

Narrative Critic

Tone is genuinely undecided — the "anxiety" pillar versus the wry Junkstronaut / "clangs on" register — and this indecision is upstream of the psychology. It must be resolved before anyone can assert how loss feels or what audience the game targets.

Player Psychologist

Builds the loss-aversion retention analysis on the assumption that "anxiety" is the settled, governing feel. If the shipped presentation leans wry, the sting of a lost full haul may land as a comic pratfall — changing the behavioral prediction, not just softening it.

Escalated The board cannot settle which tone governs from the document. Business Analyst ties it to the positioning vacuum: the two tones target opposite audiences wanting opposite art, audio, and marketing. Pick the governing tone; it gates both the retention model and the market position.

03 The Full Board

Systems Designer

Core loop, progression, pacing, difficulty curve, resource / economy math.

Blocking

The win object has no upgrade path that reaches it

Fixed non-upgradeable chute plus √mass descent puts a doubled-mass haul past the soft-landing line; no purchasable upgrade buys down touchdown speed, and staging removes powered descent.

Major

The upgrade tree doesn't touch the game's hardest system

Fuel, thruster, and storage all ease the ascent or worsen the descent; reentry — the stated central difficulty engine — gets no player-controlled counter-lever, so difficulty only ramps up with no brake.

Major

Jetpack fuel is a load-bearing resource that is never defined

It gates the collect loop and triggers a loss, yet has no stated capacity, refill rule, or relationship to tank fuel, and can't be upgraded — the economy sweep has nothing to sweep around.

Major

The economy has a floor, one anchor, and no fail state (upgraded from Minor)

A single break-even data point, no piece values or prices, and an unmodeled repair sink; upgraded to Major in Round 2 once QA's bankruptcy case showed the missing model produces an unrecoverable terminal state.

Minor

The soft-landing requirement fights the chute window for heavy loads

"Deploy early to land soft" collides with "wait out plasma," which for heavy hauls opens later and lower; a legal deploy window may not exist across the normal mass range.

Round 2 — Cross-examination

Held, strengthened: Finding 1 independently confirmed by Adversarial QA with the descent-speed arithmetic spelled out — two isolated reviewers converging on an unreachable win is the strongest signal in-lane.

Upgraded: Finding 4 raised Minor → Major after QA's bankruptcy soft-lock reframed "no downward pressure" as an unrecoverable poverty state.

Connection: Finding 1 × Feasibility's missing pilot policy — the flaw and the mechanism that would catch it are both absent. Finding 2 × QA's low-arc heat bypass lets players skip the difficulty engine for ~9 tiers of grind.

Narrative Critic

Story logic, character motivation, tone, theme, and whether the narrative pays off what it promises.

Major

The protagonist has no identity, no stake, no reason to want the ending

The win is framed as Armstrong's goal, not the player's; the junkstronaut is a blank, and the triumphant return the structure promises has no character to land on.

Major

Armstrong is written as a character but implemented as a vending machine

He is named, owns the yard, and drives the endgame, yet every point of contact is silent and transactional — no dialogue, cutscene, or delivery beat anywhere in the document.

Major

The climax may be quietly hollow: returning a damaged satellite

The satellite "arrives damaged unless the touchdown is soft," but the Win condition says nothing about condition — the emotional peak is undefined at exactly the moment it matters most.

Minor

"Starting with" promises a campaign the game doesn't deliver

The hook frames the satellite as the first objective of a larger reclamation, but it is also the single win object — the game opens and closes on the same object.

Minor

Unreconciled tone: is this anxious or a scrapyard comedy?

The "anxiety" pillar pulls against the wry Junkstronaut / blue-collar / "clangs on" register; the document never states which tone governs, leaving art and audio nothing to align to.

Round 2 — Cross-examination

Strengthened: Systems F1 and QA F1 convert Finding 3 from "the payoff is undefined" into "the mechanics may force the hollow payoff" — the damaged delivery may be the only ending the game can produce. Held at Major (the narrative fix is cheap and independent).

Tension: Player Psychologist tunes a loss-aversion critique against a tonal commitment (Finding 5) the document hasn't made — the tonal undefinedness is upstream of the psychology.

Connection: The psychologist's missing positive reinforcement and this lens's mute Armstrong (Finding 2) are the same hole — a voiced Armstrong is the obvious missing source of triumphant payoff. No withdrawals.

Player Psychologist

Moment-to-moment feel — the first hour, confusion, rage-quits, and whether the reward structure keeps players coming back.

Blocking

The hardest, most punishing mechanic has no described onboarding

No tutorial, safe first flight, practice mode, or forgiving early band for a one-way-staging reentry with permanent failure — the most likely first hour ends in destruction the player doesn't understand.

Major

Control scheme is overloaded and inconsistent across contexts

Seven verbs on non-standard keys, WASD meaning different things in rocket vs. EVA, no remapping or on-screen prompts — a single fat-finger on one-way staging or a mistimed chute is catastrophic.

Major

The reward loop is built on loss aversion, and it peaks at the worst moment

Investment and catastrophic-loss risk both peak on the full-hold descent, with no partial credit, close-call feedback, or streak reward — pushing players toward timid hauls or rage-quitting.

Major

Long-term motivation rests on a repetitive grind toward a single goal

Nine upgrade tiers through one band with thin margins, no enemies, events, variety, or narrative beats — "why do I launch a 20th time?" is unanswered.

Minor

The single most important feedback cue is a subtle color shift under time pressure

The safe-to-deploy signal is a hull glow fading white → orange — a color-only gradient with no threshold, audio, or gauge backup, a known accessibility problem where mis-timing destroys the run.

Round 2 — Cross-examination

Conflict: Contests Business Analyst's Minor rating of "feeling activates late" — combined with loss aversion the curve is boredom on the front, trauma on the back, no graduated middle: a structural retention failure, not a positioning nit.

Connection: Feasibility's missing autopilot is independent evidence for the onboarding wall (F1); QA's soft-lock and unrepaired chute upgrade "players quit" from a motivational to a mechanically enforced death spiral.

Reframe: If the endgame is unlandable, Finding 4 is not "the grind gets boring" but "the grind pays out in a broken promise." Severities held.

Feasibility Lead

Scope, buildability, and schedule for a solo 7-day Godot build driven by Claude Code agents.

Major

The Playtester needs an agent that can fly the game, and the doc never says who

Every sweep question requires piloting the ship through reentry, but no autopilot, input trace, or heuristic controller is specified — the load-bearing claim of the whole AI-architecture section rests on an unspecified component.

Major

"Deterministic" is asserted but collisions on CharacterBody2D don't guarantee it

Core mechanics ride Godot's built-in 2D collision solver, which is not contractually deterministic across runs; if a sweep diverges on contact ordering, the outcome table is untrustworthy, and no QA invariant checks reproducibility.

Major

The coupled loop backloads all integration risk

Both sweeps require nearly the whole game standing up at once, so they land at end-of-week and any single slipped mechanic blocks them — an integration cliff the doc never flags.

Major

Mechanic count vs. one-per-session cadence, and an over-clean token budget

~12+ mechanics at one-per-session with zero slack for red-test rework, and a raw-token budget stated against a clean weekly pool rather than Claude Pro's real rolling rate limits and gated Opus access.

Minor

Sweep-harness half-day timebox is inconsistent with Findings 1–2

Half a day is plausible for the config-grid runner alone — but only because the timebox silently excludes the flight-control policy and determinism guarantee that make its output meaningful.

Round 2 — Cross-examination

Reframed: Finding 1 is the enabling condition for the board's top blocker — absent a scripted pilot, the satellite blocker cannot be detected before ship by any process the document describes; hand-tuning is not a safety net for winnability.

Confidence up: QA's unspecified (possibly random) cargo-ablation order gives the determinism finding a concrete game-logic failure path beyond physics contact-ordering.

Connection: The psychologist's demanded tutorial and remapping are unbudgeted Coder sessions that worsen the mechanic-count-vs-schedule finding.

Adversarial QA

Internal contradictions, unhandled edge cases, and rule-breaking player behavior.

Blocking

The win object may be physically unlandable, and upgrades don't help

Doubling mass raises chute descent speed ≈1.41×; the chute is fixed and staging removes powered descent, so upgrades improve reach, not landability — the component that governs landing can never be improved.

Major

Economic soft-lock: bankruptcy is not a defined loss or recovery state

A hard-but-survivable landing can sell for less than launch cost; repeat it and the player is alive, un-stranded, and too poor to fly — an undefined state with no loss screen and no restart trigger.

Major

Jetpack fuel vs. tank fuel: the EVA-stranding case is unhandled

Fuel is one thing at the shop but two in play; an astronaut who runs the jetpack dry far from the ship with an unstowed hold has an undefined outcome the design actively encourages hitting.

Major

A shredded parachute is not covered by launch repairs

Repairs name only hull and heat shield; read literally, a player who shreds the chute in plasma can relaunch chute-less into a guaranteed hard landing, with no shop path to fix the root cause.

Minor

Cargo-ablation order is unspecified: exploit and/or feel-bad

Heat destroys "one slot per ~3 s" with no stated order — enabling sacrificial-panel shielding if predictable, or an invisible coin-flip loss of the run's value if random.

Round 2 — Cross-examination

Conflict, won: Against Systems and Business reading break-even as a floor — Systems' own repair-sink observation ("damaged runs are net-negative") supplies the exact downward pressure their headline denies, so the bankruptcy finding comes out strengthened, now triple-sourced.

Connection: Finding 1 × Narrative's hollow climax shows the damaged ending is the mechanically default outcome; × Feasibility's missing pilot means the sweep can't distinguish "unwinnable by design" from "autopilot too weak."

Re-clustered: The Minor ablation-order finding folds under the psychologist's loss-aversion frame as a rage-quit amplifier. Nothing withdrawn.

Business Analyst

Feature creep vs. load-bearing scope, market positioning, audience clarity, and whether the document serves several masters.

Major

The document is two documents fused; game spec is crowded out by methodology

Half the page is AI-build cadence, token counts, and GUT invariants while playtime, session length, piece count, and run-count go thin or missing — it under-specifies the game it is meant to drive.

Major

No target audience, no market positioning, no comparables

Theme and feeling are stated, but never who this is for or where it sits — no genre placement, no "X-meets-Y for players who liked Z," so feel and scope decisions have no yardstick.

Major

Content volume is thin and undefined; the whole game is one loop with a single climax

One band of functionally identical junk, one win object, no post-game, and no stated run-count — the number that decides whether the single band survives the nine-tier grind.

Minor

The marketed "feeling" is undercut by the safety nets, and only activates late

Break-even and land-anywhere selling mean the anxiety hook has teeth only under greedy late-game play; the pitch promises tension from minute one, the systems deliver it much later.

Round 2 — Cross-examination

Conceded: Withdraws "break-even is a working safety net" for "unverified reassurance" — the pitch offers a floor the economy may not provide; cedes the higher-severity economic version to QA/Systems while keeping the distinct positioning face (Finding 4 held at Minor).

Connection: Finding 3 × Player Psychologist's monotony converge on the missing run-count as the single highest-leverage addition; the positioning vacuum (F2) × Narrative's tone gap are two faces of the same undecided-audience problem.

Tension: Feasibility's "scoped defensibly for a week" answers buildability, not whether one homogeneous band survives the grind. Finding 3 strengthened.
