Eighteen of twenty checks pass and the ablation curve reproduces its own model exactly, but the ship runs out of fuel before circularising in all three bands and the cheapest descent is a single plunge at every band and load — the second of those is a rule no parameter assignment can satisfy and needs a human decision, not a retune.

The ablation surface

read the valleyThe dark line along the floor is the cheapest strategy at every entry speed. It should stay in the 1–2 skim band across the whole span; here it sits at 2.

What am I looking at?

Plate burned as a function of both inputs at once — how fast you are coming in, and how many passes you split it into. Two independent variables and one output, so this is the one place a surface beats a line chart.

The dark line is the valley floor — the cheapest pass count at every entry speed. It is the answer to the hardest question in §2.3.1, and it should stay inside 2–4 across the whole span. Where it climbs toward the back, higher bands are demanding more passes; where it runs flat, a single strategy covers every altitude.

Playtest — targets missed

targets missed9 findings, 3 blocking, 7 proposed value changes.

What am I looking at?

Flown, this config never reaches orbit — all three bands run out of fuel before circularising — and of the reentry claims that can still be checked, the parachute holds exactly while the pass-count optimum, the skim cooling, and the band difficulty ordering all fail, the last of them inverted: the high band is measured as the cheapest descent in the game.

  • blocking ascent does not close in any band (§4.1, tuning) — Verification sweep, ascents: suborbital reached=false, low reached=false, high reached=false, all three 'out of fuel before circularising'. Reported fuel_remaining 503.73 / 377.44 / 248.37 kg (28% / 21% / 13.8%). Ideal-rocket-equation dv is not what the integrator spends: the ship is losing the difference to the climb. Every descent, ablation and economy figure in the params is conditioned on an orbit the ship does not achieve. Grid: bands_reachable is satisfied by 50% of the 5,184 configs, and every top-scoring world runs fuel_fraction 2.5-4.0 against this ship's 1800/1350 = 1.33.
  • blocking cheapest descent is single pass everywhere (§2.3.1, design) — cheapest_pass_count = 1 in all nine band x load cases. Peak heat is nearly flat in pass count because braking altitude climbs as passes are added: suborbital empty 70 -> 74 while braking altitude goes 27,846 -> 38,424 m; high full hold 94 -> 108 across 22,450 -> 30,221 m. Cost is monotonically increasing from pass 1 in every case (suborbital empty 4.6 / 5.6 / 6.6 / 8.3%; high full hold 10.8 / 15.8 / 17.4 / 19.4%). The heat term has nothing to give back, so no assignment of cycle_toll_base_pct, cycle_toll_growth, heat_cost_coefficient or heat_cost_exponent moves the minimum off 1. This confirms GDD 4.5 risk 1 rather than clearing it: the U-curve does not exist inside a 43,000 m atmosphere. The decision is a human's — accept the skim framing into 2.3.1, or take the named fallback of flat per-pass ablation with a hard pass cap. Neither is a number I can nudge.
  • blocking difficulty curve inverts with altitude (§2.3.7, tuning) — Flown, the ordering reverses. Cheapest ablation, empty: suborbital 4.6%, low 4.0%, high 3.6%. Full hold: 13.5% / 11.7% / 10.8%. Peak heat on the cheapest descent, empty: 70 / 67 / 64. The high band is the cheapest and coolest return in the game at every cargo load — the exact inversion GDD 4.5 risk 2 is written against, and it is measured, not predicted. (Held at a fixed entry depth the ordering is correct — see heat_index_high_overstated — so the inversion is produced by the descent solver braking higher bands deeper, not by the heat model.) Grid: difficulty_rises_with_band is satisfied by 60.8% of configs, so this is reachable; GDD 4.5 names the lever as band-gated ablation rates, which is what ablation.heat_index already is.
  • significant skimming buys almost nothing at this ballistic coefficient (§2.3.1, tuning) — High band, entry_depth 0: [1, 0.99, 0.98, 0.972] — three skims buy 2.8%, a third less than the adopted array, and at entry_depth 21,500 m it degrades to [1, 0.994, 0.988, 0.983], 1.7%. Each skim costs commit_dv 44-45 m/s and 0.53 h of flight time. Entry depth by contrast moves heat 159.9 -> 90.9 (43% cooler) over the same 21,500 m — roughly 20x the leverage of the whole skim stack. The controlling quantity is the ballistic coefficient: the sweep reports staged 290.3 kg/m2, while the grid worlds where skimming genuinely cools run beta 33-133 and reach [1, 0.925, 0.849, 0.766] or better. Grid: skimming_cools_the_entry satisfied by 43.8% of configs, so this is tuning, not an impossible rule.
  • significant optimal skims high is one not two (§2.3.1, tuning) — Re-running the params' own arithmetic on the measured multipliers [1, 0.99, 0.98, 0.972] at heat_index 138: heat terms 32.85 / 31.88 / 30.92 / 30.17, tolls 0.28 / 0.784 / 1.691 / 3.324, skim heat 0 / 0.133 / 0.266 / 0.399, totals 33.13 / 32.80 / 32.88 / 33.89. The minimum moves to 1 skim, and 0 vs 1 are separated by 0.33 plate-percent. At the corrected heat_index of 128 the totals are 26.49 / 26.36 / 26.63 / 27.79 — still 1. The optimum is inside the required 1-2 window either way, but the stated value is wrong and the margin is thinner than a rounding error on the plate gauge.
  • minor heat index high overstated not understated (§2.3.1, tuning) — It is routed, and it points the other way. At a fixed entry depth of 0 with no skims, an empty ship peaks at 100 (suborbital) / 125 (low) / 159.9 (high) on the sweep's bar, which is normalised to suborbital = 100. Renormalised to the params' own convention of low = 100, the measured indices are 80 / 100 / 127.9 against the stated 78 / 100 / 138. The high band is 7.9% overstated; suborbital is 2.6% understated. The 161.5 figure the note was reacting to is on the suborbital-normalised scale, which is what made it look 17% low.
  • minor parachute claim holds exactly (§2.3.2, tuning) — Independently measured at 4.59 m/s, delta 0, spread across bands 0. Touchdown is 3.22 / 3.96 / 4.59 m/s at empty / half / full hold and soft_landing = true in all nine descents. full_hold_mass_ratio measured 2.04 against the claimed 2.035, inside the 1.75-2.25 window. This is the one claim in the file the simulator confirms to the digit. Worth knowing that the margin is 0.41 m/s and that only 31.5% of grid configs satisfy full_hold_lands_soft at all — storage tier 1 spends most of what is left.
  • minor unstaged pass survives here and almost nowhere else (§2.3.1, design) — Holds in this world: shallow unstaged pass peaks at 54 (suborbital) and 60 (low), both survivable against a 100 capacity. But the grid satisfies unstaged_pass_survivable in only 7.4% of 5,184 configs, and every one of the twelve top-scoring worlds fails it, at unstaged peak heats of 356-419. This is the one target the crew's config wins and the grid's best worlds lose, which is the reason not to adopt a top-scoring grid world wholesale.
  • minor reported ballistic coefficient is at dry mass (§2.3.5, tuning) — The sweep reports staged 290.3 and unstaged 967.7 kg/m2. Both back-solve to 1,350 kg (290.3 x 1.5 x 3.1 and 967.7 x 0.45 x 3.1), so the reported figure is the dry, empty, fuel-less ship — not the same quantity the params quote. The two do not disagree about physics; the descents plainly do carry cargo mass, since peak heat moves 70 -> 102 from empty to full hold. Both figures sit far above the ~50 kg/m2 floor below which aerobraking cannot exist, so the mechanic is intact — but any beta comparison between the params and the sweep is currently comparing two different ships.

What the simulator cannot tell you
  • The ascent finding is load-bearing and the sweep is internally inconsistent about it: it reports 'out of fuel before circularising' alongside 503.73 kg remaining and a 28% margin. Both cannot be literal. I trust reached=false — it is the flag the descent cases are all conditioned on — and I would not quote the 28% figure to anyone until someone says what budget it is a margin against.
  • The descent series and the skim series disagree in sign about band difficulty: descents get cooler with altitude (70 / 67 / 64) while fixed-depth entries get hotter (100 / 125 / 159.9). The difference is entry depth, which the descent solver floats and the skim series pins. This is exactly the confusion the charter warns about, so I have kept them apart: the inversion finding is about what the descent solver does, and the heat_index finding is about what the heat model does. Neither should be quoted as evidence for the other.
  • The Researcher predicts a 1.31x band-to-band heat spread from v-cubed at a common periapsis; the simulator measures 1.60x at a common entry depth. I have not reconciled these and I would not assume the simulator's heat model is the Researcher's. The heat bar is a normalised 0-100 scale by the sweep's own statement, so every heat figure I have quoted is relative, and none of it is W/m2.
  • The fuel and thrust proposals are derived from the grid's fuel_fraction and TWR distributions plus the rocket equation, not from a flown re-run of this ship. They are the direction the evidence points, not a measured fix, and they must be re-swept before anyone believes the specific numbers. They also invalidate the fuel_tank and thruster upgrade ladders, which is the economy-balancer's work and not mine.
  • The reference_area change moves the quantity that controls whether skimming exists at all, but 6.0 m2 is chosen to land near the grid's cooling band, not measured. It also raises unstaged drag, which currently has comfortable margin (54 and 60 against a 100 capacity) but is satisfied by only 7.4% of the grid — if the re-sweep shows unstaged peaks climbing toward 100, this is the change to back off first.
  • The simulator is a point mass with a perfect pilot. off_retrograde_penalty, the cosine heat rule, chute-shred timing against the plasma cue, latch and swing jerk, debris collisions, and everything in the tether model are untested by anything I have seen — no finding above rests on them, and the cryo_propellant_sphere concern the economy-balancer raised remains open, not cleared.
  • None of the top-scoring grid worlds is a drop-in replacement. They vary planet radius and gravity, which the design has fixed elsewhere, and all twelve fail unstaged_pass_survivable at peaks of 356-419 where the crew's config sits at 54-60. I have used the grid only to separate tuning problems from design ones, which is what the satisfaction rates are for.
  • I cannot say whether any of this is fun. The plasma-to-chute window, whether a 0.41 m/s parachute margin reads as tense or as luck, and whether one skim feels like a decision or like arithmetic are the human's checkpoints 2 and 3, and nothing in the sweep touches them.

What am I looking at?

Eighteen of twenty checks pass and the ablation curve reproduces its own model exactly, but the ship runs out of fuel before circularising in all three bands and the cheapest descent is a single plunge at every band and load — the second of those is a rule no parameter assignment can satisfy and needs a human decision, not a retune.

cheapest_descent_is_multi_pass — GDD §2.3.1
The cheapest descent is 2-4 committed passes, never a single plunge and never feathering.
Found: Judged on flown pass counts only. descents[].cheapest_pass_count = 1 in all nine band x load cases. The series are monotonically increasing from the first pass in every one: suborbital empty 4.6 / 5.6 / 6.6 / 8.3 / 11.3 / 16.6 / 26.1 / 43.3%; low full hold 11.7 / 14.9 / 16.5 / 18.3 / 21.3 / 27.7%; high full hold 10.8 / 15.8 / 17.4 / 19.4 / 22.6 / 28.3%. The failure is a single plunge, not feathering. The reason is visible in the same tables: peak heat is almost flat in pass count because braking altitude climbs as passes are added — suborbital empty 70 -> 74 while braking altitude goes 27,846 -> 38,424 m; high full hold 94 -> 108 across 22,450 -> 30,221 m. A heat term that gives back 4-14 points of peak cannot pay for a cycle toll that compounds at 1.8x, so no assignment of cycle_toll_base_pct, cycle_toll_growth, heat_cost_coefficient or heat_cost_exponent moves the minimum off 1 — lowering the toll flattens the curve toward pass 1, raising the heat term steepens it toward pass 1. Inside a 43,000 m atmosphere at this ballistic coefficient the U-curve does not exist. This is not a value the Balancer chose badly; it is a rule the parameters cannot satisfy.
shipping_slice_bands_reachable — GDD §4.1
Every band the shipping slice needs — suborbital and low — is reachable, and the catalog populates both with at least one fragile piece and at least two size classes.
Found: The catalog half holds: suborbital carries 2 fragile types (cracked_antenna_whip, shattered_lens_cowl) across 3 size classes (small, medium, oversized) and low carries 3 fragile types (cracked_solar_array, star_tracker_head, parabolic_dish_section) across the same 3, so the player meets crushing and fragility inside the slice. The reachability half does not: the simulator reports ascents suborbital reached = false, low reached = false, high reached = false, all three 'out of fuel before circularising' with 503.73 / 377.44 / 248.37 kg remaining (28% / 21% / 13.8% short). The params claim 2,542 m/s of ideal dv from exhaust velocity 78000/26 = 3000 m/s and mass ratio 3150/1350 = 2.333 (3000 * ln 2.333 = 2542), against roughly 1450 and 1800 m/s of orbital requirement — but the ideal rocket equation is not what the integrator spends, and gravity losses on the climb consume the margin. Fuel fraction is 1800/1350 = 1.33 where the grid's top-scoring worlds run 2.5-4.0. Every descent, ablation, landing and economy figure in this file is conditioned on an orbit the ship does not achieve.
Observations — pass the spec, worth a human's eye
  • The difficulty curve is inverted in flight, and this is the finding a human should read first after the ascent failure. Cheapest ablation empty is 4.6% (suborbital) / 4.0% (low) / 3.6% (high) and full hold is 13.5% / 11.7% / 10.8%; peak heat on the cheapest descent empty is 70 / 67 / 64. The high band is the cheapest and coolest return in the game at every load, against GDD 2.3.7's 'the value curve and the risk curve rise together'. The cost_curve asserts the opposite ordering (6.21 / 12.78 / 32.03) and passes its own check, so nothing in the audit's rule list catches this — the curve is indexed by skims and the inversion lives in the descent solver, which brakes higher bands deeper. Held at a fixed entry depth the ordering is correct (100 / 125 / 159.9 peak), so the heat model is not what is wrong.
  • The skim decision is arithmetic, not a feeling. In the high band 0.28 plate-percent separates 1 skim from 2 (32.31 vs 32.03) and 0.08 separates 0 from 1 in the low band. A player cannot perceive a third of a percent on a plate gauge, so the mechanic the GDD sells as a judgement call reads as a lookup table. Substituting the measured multipliers moves the high-band optimum from 2 to 1 and the separation to 0.33 — the stated optimal_skims.high = 2 is wrong against flight, even though the window it must land in is satisfied either way.
  • Skimming is dominated by a knob the design does not mention. Three skims buy 2.8% of peak heat at entry depth 0 and 1.7% at 21,500 m, costing 44-45 m/s of commit dv and 0.53 h each; entry depth alone moves high-band peak heat 159.9 -> 90.9 (43% cooler) over that same 21,500 m. Whatever the player is actually optimising, it is not skim count. The controlling quantity is the ballistic coefficient — staged 290.3 kg/m2 here against 33-133 in the grid worlds where skimming genuinely cools.
  • heat_index.high 138 is overstated by about 8%, not understated by 17% as the balance notes worried. Renormalised to the params' own low = 100 convention the measured indices are 80 / 100 / 127.9. Because the entry term is cubed, a 7.9% index error is a 26% error in the dominant cost of every high-band descent, and cost_curve.high would move with it.
  • The two ballistic coefficients in circulation describe different ships and should not be compared again without saying which. The params quote 591 kg/m2 (2747.8 kg full hold over Cd 1.5 x 3.1 m2); the sim reports 290.3, which back-solves to 1350 kg — dry, empty and fuel-less. Both sit far above the ~50 kg/m2 floor below which aerobraking cannot exist, so the mechanic is intact either way, but the reference area is held at 3.1 m2 across every heat-shield tier, which cannot be right for a plate you pay to upgrade and is the cheapest lever if the descents fly long.
  • The economy has no slack at either end. launch_cost 120 is exactly three cheap pieces, the top of its permitted window rather than inside it, and the full-hold parachute margin is 0.41 m/s of which storage tier 1 spends 0.37 (4.59 -> 4.96 m/s). Both pass, and both fail on the next revision that moves a mass anywhere.
  • fragile_value_premium 3.0 is thin once the uncrushable slot cost is charged. A fragile small occupies 2 slots forever against a solid small's 1, so the per-slot premium is 1.5x, not 3x — and the fragile piece additionally takes double reentry and landing damage. GDD 2.3.7 wants one recovered intact to reshape a run's payout; at 1.5x per slot the rational player who is not certain of a soft landing skips the glint, which wastes the mechanic.
  • dented_pressure_bottle at 380 kg in one crushed slot is the single piece that sets the shipping slice's 232.96 kg per slot, and therefore the piece that forced dry_mass_kg from 850 to 1350 to keep the doubling true. The base ship's mass is currently a function of one debris entry.
Catalog concerns raised by the Economy Balancer

Not audit findings. These are problems the Balancer hit while pricing the catalog and could not fix, because it does not own that data. On a failing run they are routed to the Debris Designer; on a passing run they land here.

  • dented_pressure_bottle, mass_kg 380 in size_class small (1 crushed slot). It is the piece most responsible for the flown 232.96 kg per crushed slot, which is what forced dry_mass_kg from 850 to 1,350 to keep the GDD 2.3.5 doubling true. The catalog is self-consistent and I balanced against it as it stands, but the Designer should know that a 380 kg one-slot piece sets the per-slot mass of the whole shipping slice, and that the base ship's dry mass is now a function of it.
  • cryo_propellant_sphere, mass_kg 2200 in size_class medium, hand-tetherable. At base magnet_hold_force_n 900 N steady towing is safe by a wide margin (228.6 N), but latch jerk scales with closing speed times piece mass, so this piece defines the high band's shear behaviour on its own — it is 1.9x the next heaviest hand-tetherable piece. That is a sweep target rather than a defect, but if the Playtester finds latch jerk unforgiving at any magnet tier, this is the piece it will be about.
