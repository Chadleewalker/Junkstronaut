where the curve bottoms outCheapest descent is 1 skim from the lower band, 1 skim from the mid band, 1 skim from the upper band. A single-pass optimum here is expected and is not a defect — the plunge is always the cheapest way down, and the design does not try to out-price it. The requirement that a heavy haul cannot plunge is checked on the pass axis, against the heat capacity, and it holds.

The ablation surface

read the valleyThe dark line along the floor is the cheapest strategy at every entry speed. It reports where the model bottoms out; here it sits at 1 from the upper band.

What am I looking at?

Plate burned as a function of both inputs at once — how fast you are coming in, and how many passes you split it into. Two independent variables and one output, so this is the one place a surface beats a line chart.

The dark line is the valley floor — the cheapest pass count at every entry speed. It is the answer to the hardest question in §2.3.1, and it should stay inside 2–4 across the whole span. Where it climbs toward the back, higher altitudes are demanding more passes; where it runs flat, a single strategy covers every altitude.

Playtest — targets missed

targets missed11 findings, 1 blocking, 5 proposed value changes.

What am I looking at?

The config flies, but the endgame's whole gate — base heat capacity above a full hold's plunge and below the module's best skimmed descent — is a measured window only 8.8 bar units wide (180.1 to 188.9), narrower than the skim-altitude scan's own noise, and three params claims (optimal_skims, cost_curve, full-hold descent speed) are contradicted by the flight.

  • blocking endgame capacity window is 8 8 bar units (§2.2, tuning) — Flown from the top of the band at commit_floor_m 8000: full hold (805.5 kg) plunges at 180.1 and the module's cheapest skimmed descent is 188.9 (2 skims; 265.3 plunge, 203.4 at 1 skim, 200.9 at 3). The base capacity must exceed 180.1 and stay under 188.9 — a window of 8.8 units, 4.9%. 185 clears the full hold by 2.7% and undercuts the module by 2.1%. The heaviest ordinary piece (828 kg) plunges at 181.0, 2.2% under the bar. The balancer's 23%/7.7% margins were computed against a 104.1 kg hold; the simulator flew 805.5 kg.
  • significant full hold doubling excludes the endgame gate (§2.4, design) — The sweep's own hold model gives kg_per_slot 134.25, full_hold_kg 805.5, full_hold_mass_ratio 1.58 — against the params' 17.357 kg/slot and 1.074. Neither reaches 2.0, and making it true is worse than leaving it: top-of-band plunge peak measures 144.7 at 0 kg, 163.5 at 402.8 kg, 180.1 at 805.5 kg and 265.3 at 3,600 kg, so a doubling hold (~1,400 kg of cargo) extrapolates to roughly 200 bar units on a plunge — above the module's best skimmed descent of 188.9. No heat capacity can then permit the full hold's plunge and forbid the module's. The two claims are mutually exclusive at any dry mass.
  • significant optimal skims and cost curve contradicted by flight (§2.2, tuning) — cheapest_pass_count is 1 pass (i.e. zero skims, a plunge) in all 15 flown descent cells, at every altitude and every load including the 3,600 kg endgame haul. The flown plate cost for an empty ship at the bottom runs [7.6, 11.6, 16.0, 20.9] against the emitted [12.10, 10.39, 16.59, 23.52]: the plunge is overstated by 4.5 points, the one-skim descent understated by 1.2, and the argmin is inverted. The flight agrees with GDD Rev-3's own statement that one pass is cheapest under every ablation keying; the params are the outlier, inherited from pricing the bottom sample off the top sample's 0.434 multiplier when the bottom's own measured value is 0.700.
  • significant full hold descent speed claim off by 21 percent (§2.3, tuning) — 4.83 m/s, delta 0.84 (21%), spread 0.0 across all three sample altitudes. The canopy is not implicated — the sweep flew the stated 630 m2 at Cd 1.8 independently — the disagreement is entirely the hold: 2,205.5 kg all-up against the params' 1,504.1. The design rule still passes (4.83 < 5.0 soft line) with 3.4% of margin instead of 20%, and empty lands at 3.85.
  • significant altitude difficulty gradient exists only because of the commit floor (§2.6, design) — It does not rise on its own. With entry depth free, an empty ship's cheapest descent gets *easier* with altitude: 7.6% of plate and peak 70 from the bottom, 7.2% and 67 from the middle, 6.9% and 65 from the top — the planner simply picks a better braking altitude from a higher, slower orbit. Only with the entry pinned at commit_floor_m 8000 does the gradient invert to the designed direction: 93.2 / 114.2 / 144.7. The commit floor is not just the endgame's gate, it is the sole source of the campaign's difficulty curve, which is a stronger reason for its never-cut status than the GDD currently gives.
  • significant top of band unreachable at base config and no upgraded ship was flown (§2.5, tuning) — Bottom and middle confirm the balancer exactly: arc reached with 13.3% margin and a 125.9 s EVA window, and 5.1% at the middle (which also settles GDD 4.5's open 97 s question upward, to 125.9 s). The top sample fails outright — 'crashed on ascent', arc apex 192,203 m against a 215,000 m sample, 10.6% short, with climb_peak_heat logged as 0. Every top-of-band descent in this sweep was therefore flown from an altitude the flown ship cannot reach, and no upgraded flight configuration (tank, shield or parachute tier) was flown at all, so §3's questions 6 and 8 — module infeasible-as-plunge at every shield tier, module run beatable at full upgrades — remain unmeasured.
  • significant module lands hard on every canopy flown (§2.6, tuning) — The module touches down at 7.28 m/s from all three sample altitudes on the base 630 m2 canopy — 46% over the 5.0 soft line, in a descent the sweep itself grades soft_landing false. Since the sweep flies a perfect pilot with no flare error, a competently-flown module delivery on the starter canopy loses a state on touchdown regardless of heat, so pristine is unreachable without parachute tier 2 (1,400 m2, unflown; predicted 4.88 m/s). This makes the parachute track, not the shield track, the gate on the triumph ending — which the GDD does not say anywhere.
  • minor ordinary cargo never lands hard (§2.5, design) — Across the entire ordinary load range the touchdown speed moves 3.85 (empty) → 4.37 (half) → 4.83 (full) → 4.86 (heaviest ordinary piece, 828 kg), a 1.01 m/s spread that never crosses the 5.0 line. Only the 3,600 kg module (7.28) lands hard. At this canopy the mass scale has one zone for everything a player can actually haul, and greed is paid for in plate (7.6% → 10.4% at the bottom) and heat (70 → 90), not in landings.
  • minor starter shield lesson is 20 percent of the bar (§4.4, tuning) — The climb peaks at 37.5 (bottom route) and 36.0 (middle) against reentry.heat_capacity 185 — 20.3% of the bar, a fifth rather than a half. The obvious fix is blocked: capacity 185 is pinned inside the 180.1–188.9 endgame window (finding 1) and cannot be lowered to sharpen the lesson without deleting the gate. The lever has to be the climb's heat, not the bar's capacity.
  • minor skim peak constant has no measured support (§2.2, tuning) — One is now reported and it is zero: skim_peak_heat is 0.0 in every row of every skim study at all three samples (bottom scanned to 27,950 m, middle 25,800 m, top 23,650 m), while entry_peak_heat matches ablation.heat_index exactly (100 / 125 / 160, delta 0.000). Either skims deposit no bar heat in this model or the field is unpopulated; in both readings 46.7 is unsupported, and the per-skim heat term is roughly 12% of a one-skim descent's emitted cost.
  • minor unstaged pass survives here but almost nowhere in the grid (§2.2, design) — On this planet the choice exists: a shallow unstaged pass peaks at 52 (bottom) and 58 (middle) against a 185 bar, comfortably survivable at reentry.unstaged_heat_multiplier 3. Across the 5,184-cell world grid the same target holds in only 8.0% of configurations, and all twelve of the grid's best configurations (score 8 of 9) fail it — no world in the grid scores 9. The mechanic is a property of the locked planet, not of the rule, so it should not be traded away for anything, and the top of the band was never flown unstaged.

What the simulator cannot tell you
  • The skim-altitude scan is coarser than the margins finding 1 turns on. Two committed series are non-monotonic in skim count — middle/full hold reads 143.0, 108.2, 118.2, 105.2 and top/empty reads 144.7, 83.6, 102.3, 108.8 — so the scan resolves peak heat to something like plus or minus 10-12 bar units. That is larger than the 8.8-unit endgame window itself. Treat any capacity margin under roughly 15 units as unresolved rather than measured, and re-fly the top/endgame column at a finer skim-altitude resolution before trusting 188.9 as the number the shield ladder is built against.
  • Two hold models are live in this crew and they differ by 7.7x: the params compute 17.357 kg per slot from the catalog's crushed/uncrushed slot table, the simulator computes 134.25. Every heat, plate and touchdown figure I quote is on the simulator's model. Which one is correct is a catalog question I cannot settle from here, and it decides whether the endgame window is 4.9% or roughly 30% wide — it is the single highest-value thing for the auditor to resolve.
  • The simulator is a point mass with a perfect pilot: no attitude error, no flare, no debris collisions, and EVA is not flown at all. The 125.9 s arc window at the band floor is time above the floor, not a demonstration that a piece can be reached, tethered and stowed inside it; GDD 4.5's open question survives this sweep. Likewise the module's 7.28 m/s touchdown assumes an optimal chute deploy, so it is a floor on how hard the module lands, not an average.
  • No upgraded ship was flown. Tank tier 1 and 2, shield tiers 215 and 245, and parachute tiers 900 and 1400 m2 appear in my findings only through interpolation from base-config flights, and the top-of-band ascent failed outright at base (apex 192,203 m). The finale's feasibility at full upgrades — §3's headline question — is still unmeasured, and one upgraded-config sweep would answer more than any re-tune of the numbers I did measure.
  • The exploration grid's satisfaction rates are a statement about the target set, not a menu of alternative worlds: its best-scoring configurations are 800 m-radius planets with 224 m of atmosphere and staged ballistic coefficients as low as 11.9 kg/m2, well under the 50 kg/m2 floor below which aerobraking cannot exist. planet.lock.json forbids moving the world in any case, so none of my proposals touch baseline.json. What the rates are good for is the classification: skimming_cools_the_entry at 84.6% and skim_benefit_saturates at 88.9% say the skim mechanic is robust and its shortfalls here are tuning; unstaged_pass_survivable at 8.0% says that one is a property of this planet and must not be traded.
  • I believe the simulator, not the params, on skim_peak: the flown study reports skim_peak_heat as 0.0 in every row at every sample. But zero is also what an unpopulated field looks like, so I have flagged 46.7 as unsupported rather than proposing a replacement. If the field is real, the cost curve is overcharging each skim by 1.96% of plate; if it is a gap in the harness, the constant is untested and should be measured before the next audit prices anything off it.

parachute_speed_matches_flight — GDD §2.3
The claimed full-hold descent speed is what the stated canopy actually flies.
Found: parachute.independent is true — landing.parachute_area_m2 630 and parachute_drag_coefficient 1.8 are both stated in the params and were flown as stated, so this is a real measurement and not a tautology. claimed_full_hold_ms 3.99 against measured_full_hold_ms 4.83, delta_ms 0.84 = 21.1% of the claim, against a 5% tolerance (0.20 m/s). The canopy is not implicated: sqrt(2*2,205.5*9/(1.5*1.8*630)) = sqrt(23.339) = 4.83 m/s reproduces the measurement exactly, so the entire disagreement is the mass the claim was computed on — 1,504.1 kg all-up in balance_notes (a 104.1 kg hold) against 2,205.5 kg flown (an 805.5 kg hold).
Observations — pass the spec, worth a human's eye
  • The harness's hold is 42% lighter than the catalog's own: cargo.kg_per_slot 134.25 and full_hold_kg 805.5, against 231.89 kg/slot spawn-weighted over the hand-tetherable shipping slice and 1,391 kg for six slots — which is the number GDD 2.2 flew at ~1,398 kg. Every full-hold figure in this sweep is therefore optimistic, and two passing checks are sensitive to it. Extrapolating the flown mass-to-peak curve (144.7 at 1,400 kg all-up, 180.1 at 2,205.5, exponent 0.49) puts the catalog's full hold at roughly 204 bar units on a top-of-band plunge — above heat_capacity 185 and above the module's best skimmed descent of 188.9, which would invert the third leg of heavy_descent_requires_multi_pass. The same canopy at 2,791 kg all-up gives sqrt(2*2,791*9/1,701) = 5.43 m/s, over the 5.0 soft line. Nothing in either agent's artifact can reconcile this; it is the hold model in the harness that needs to agree with shipping_slice.slots_per_piece before the next sweep is trusted.
  • The base capacity's margin over the loads it must let plunge is 2-3%, not the 32% GDD 2.2 says it is building on. From the top of the band, full hold 180.1 and the heaviest single ordinary piece (828 kg) 181.0 against heat_capacity 185. GDD 2.2 explicitly rejects a 4%-wide window as 'a coincidence and not a mechanic'; this is of that order, and it is where the hold-mass disagreement above lands first.
