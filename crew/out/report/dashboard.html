Value costs mass

The design's central bet (§2.3.7): the better the haul, the harder the ride home. If this cloud trends up and to the right, the bet holds — the valuable pieces are the heavy ones. A flat cloud means upgrades will outrun difficulty.

The ablation curve

The hardest constraint in the document (§2.3.1). One screaming plunge is expensive; a dozen feather-light passes are expensive; the cheapest descent should be a planned 2–4 committed passes. Each ring marks where that band's curve bottoms out.

  • Suborbital — cheapest at 0 skims as designed
  • Low orbit — cheapest at 1 skim as designed
  • High orbit — cheapest at 2 skims as designed

Is any size class strictly best?

Value per cargo slot. If one class towers over the others at every band, the greed decision collapses — the player just learns the rule and stops choosing.

The ablation surface

Plate burned as a function of both inputs at once — how fast you are coming in, and how many passes you split it into. Two independent variables and one output, so this is the one place a surface beats a line chart.

The dark line is the valley floor — the cheapest pass count at every entry speed. It is the answer to the hardest question in §2.3.1, and it should stay inside 2–4 across the whole span. Where it climbs toward the back, higher bands are demanding more passes; where it runs flat, a single strategy covers every altitude.

Where the design's targets are reachable at all

324 worlds, varying gravity, air density, atmosphere thickness, ship frontal area and dry mass. Each scored against seven targets taken from the design document. A short bar means almost no configuration anywhere satisfies that target — which is a fact about the rule, not about the current numbers.

Best world found: 7/8 targets — gravity 3 m/s2, air density 0.5 kg/m3, scale height 1680 m, frontal area 12 m2, dry mass 800 kg (ballistic coefficient 44.4 kg/m2, full hold touching down at 4.49 m/s).

Playtest — targets missed

Flown, this config lands softly from every band at every load and never finds a reason to skim — the cheapest descent is always a single committed pass, ascent fails outright at all three bands, and the params' heat-index and pass-count claims are contradicted by the simulator.

  • blocking ascent fails every band (§2.3.6, tuning) — The verification sweep flew all three ascents and all three read reached=false, why='crashed on ascent', fuel_remaining_kg 0, fuel_margin_pct 0 — suborbital (102500 m), low (170000 m) and high (275000 m) alike. Zero of three bands are reachable as configured, against a design that needs at least two. In the exploration grid bands_reachable is satisfied by 57.7% of the 324 worlds and fuel_margin_sane by 32.7%, so the space of working ascents is large and this is a wrong-numbers problem, not an impossible one.
  • blocking ablation optimum is one pass (§2.3.1, design) — Every one of the nine flown descents reports cheapest_pass_count = 1. The measured ablation curve is monotonically increasing in pass count with no interior minimum anywhere: suborbital full hold 10.0 / 11.6 / 12.4 / 13.0 / 13.6 / 14.2 / 14.8; low full hold 8.7 / 11.1 / 12.1 / 12.8 / 13.4; high full hold 7.7 / 11.3 / 12.3 / 13.0 / 13.8. The second pass costs 1.6-3.6 points of plate and buys nothing. The params' claimed high-band curve (39.33 / 37.33 / 36.54) is contradicted both in shape and in absolute level — the simulator's high-band figures are 7.7-13.0%, roughly a fifth of the claim.
  • blocking skimming barely cools the entry (§2.3.1, design) — At the shallowest entry depth the measured multipliers are high [1, 0.971, 0.944, 0.918], low [1, 0.956, 0.912, 0.867], suborbital [1, 0.898, 0.730, 0.730]. Two skims from the high band remove 5.6% of peak heat (157.1 -> 148.4) and cost 47 m/s of commit burn plus 1.8 extra hours of orbit time. Entry depth is 5-10x the stronger lever over the same range: high band 157.1 at depth 0 versus 100.6 at depth 39000, a 36% reduction from one choice. Skimming is therefore real but negligible, and the design's central descent decision is being made by a variable the params do not expose. Across the grid skimming_cools_the_entry is satisfiable in 43.8% of worlds, so a stronger skim exists — every grid config that gets it (multipliers down to 0.262-0.363) does so at ballistic_coefficient_staged 44.4, below the ~50 kg/m2 floor where the atmosphere stops the ship instead of braking it.
  • significant heat index understates flown heat headroom (§2.3.1, tuning) — The heat-index numbers are reproduced exactly by the skim sweep (suborbital 100, low 126.6, high 157.1 at entry_depth 0), so the normalisation is honest. But those are the shallowest-entry figures, and the flown descents choose their own braking altitude: measured peak heat for a single-pass descent is 70 (suborbital empty), 98 (suborbital full), 67 / 93 (low), 64 / 89 (high). Every flown descent peaks below reentry.heat_capacity 100 at base gear, and plate_survives is true in all nine. The upgrade gating the params derive from heat_index does not exist in flight — nothing forces heat_shield t1 or t2, and the high band is the coolest single-pass entry in the set (64 empty) because a deeper committed entry brakes earlier.
  • blocking difficulty falls with altitude (§2.3.7, design) — Flown, it inverts. Cheapest ablation by band at equal load: empty 4.1 (suborbital) / 3.6 (low) / 3.2 (high); half hold 6.8 / 5.9 / 5.2; full hold 10.0 / 8.7 / 7.7. Peak heat follows: 98 / 93 / 89 at full hold. Returning from the high band is 23% cheaper in plate and 9 points cooler than returning from suborbital, in every load case. Mass still works as designed — full hold costs 2.4x the plate of empty at high (7.7 vs 3.2) — so the greed axis holds while the altitude axis runs backwards. In the grid difficulty_rises_with_band is satisfied by only 38.6% of worlds, the second-rarest target after unstaged_pass_survivable, and the Researcher's own note predicted this: band entry speeds spread only 864 -> 954 m/s, giving high just 1.35x the flux of suborbital before any other effect.
  • significant unstaged shallow pass not survivable (§2.3.1, design) — unstaged_braking reports suborbital shallow_pass_peak_heat 407 against heat_capacity 100, survivable=false; low reads 93, survivable=true. The intended descent shape is therefore unavailable from the band the player starts in — the suborbital hull cooks at 4x capacity on the pass the GDD calls a feather. Across the grid unstaged_pass_survivable is the rarest target of the eight at 13.0%, and the top-scoring worlds split on it: every config that buys strong skimming loses it (unstaged_peak_heat 339-439) and every config that keeps it has near-flat skim multipliers (0.958-0.987). The two rules are in tension across the whole space, not just at these values.
  • minor full hold lands soft with no pressure (§2.3.2, tuning) — Measured touchdown is 2.96 m/s empty, 3.54 half hold, 4.04 full hold — identical across all three bands, and soft_landing=true in all nine descents. The full-hold figure is 4.04 against the claimed 4.18, and it sits 19% under the 5 m/s line rather than against it. The sweep also flags that landing.parachute_area_m2 is absent from the params, so the model solved for the 610.5 m2 that reproduces the 4.18 claim — the measured speeds are anchored to the crew's own assertion rather than independent of it, and the params should state the area.
  • significant ballistic coefficient sits above the braking band (§4.5, tuning) — Flown beta is 228.6 kg/m2 staged and 685.7 unstaged, a 3.0x staging benefit against the baseline's claimed 4.3x. Staged beta above ~200 is why a skim removes so little: the grid's only configs with strong skim multipliers (0.262-0.363) run beta 44.4, below the ~50 kg/m2 floor where first contact removes the whole orbit. The usable window between 'skim does nothing' and 'skim ends the orbit' is narrow in this world's geometry, and reference_area_m2 3.5 with dry_mass_kg 1200 puts the ship at the wrong end of it.

What the simulator cannot tell you
  • The ascent failures are the least trustworthy result in the set and the most consequential. All three read the same way — crashed, zero fuel, zero margin — which is the signature of either a genuine delta-v shortfall or a simulator ascent guidance that flies one fixed profile badly. The economy agent's rocket-equation arithmetic (1666 m/s against a ~1350 m/s requirement) says the margin is thin but not absent. My proposed tank and thrust changes are sized to survive either explanation, but if the ascent model is at fault they overshoot and fuel_tank t1 becomes nearly pointless. Fly one ascent by hand before spending 480 kg of tank on it.
  • descent_speed_full_hold_ms is not an independent measurement. The sweep states it inferred parachute_area_m2 = 610.5 by solving for the crew's own 4.18 m/s claim, so the measured 4.04 m/s tests internal consistency of the mass scaling, not the chute. Treat the full_hold_lands_soft finding as low-confidence until the area is authored.
  • The difficulty_falls_with_altitude finding is robust to the simulator's limits — it appears in all nine descents, in both plate cost and peak heat, and the Researcher independently predicted it from entry speeds alone (864/906/954 m/s giving only 1.35x flux spread). But the flown inversion is larger than entry speed can explain, and I believe the cause is that the descent controller picks a deeper braking altitude from higher orbits (45030 m at high full hold versus 51295 m at suborbital), which brakes earlier and cooler. That is a pilot policy, not a physical law, and a human flying a shallow high-band entry would get a hotter one. The design conclusion stands; the exact magnitudes do not.
  • The simulator is a point mass with a perfect pilot. It has no attitude error, so GDD 2.3.1's cosine off-retrograde penalty is untested and reentry.off_retrograde_penalty 1 is an unflown number. It has no debris collisions, no EVA, and no tether physics — every claim in the params about magnet shear, tether slots, jetpack handling and stow costs is unmeasured by this sweep and I make no finding on any of it. It also cannot say whether a descent with no reason to skim feels boring, which is exactly the judgement GDD 3.3 checkpoint 3 reserves for the human.
  • My reference_area_m2 proposal is the one change I am least able to bound. The sweep shows beta is the controlling quantity for whether skimming exists, and shows the working window is narrow (44.4 gives real skims, 228.6 gives none), but it never flew an intermediate beta on this planet — the 44.4 configs are all 60 km worlds. 123 kg/m2 is interpolation, not measurement, and it should be swept before it is committed.
  • unstaged_pass_survivable at 13.0% across 324 worlds is the strongest evidence in either sweep that a GDD rule is unachievable rather than untuned, and it is in direct tension with skimming strength: in the top-12 list, every world with a skim multiplier below 0.4 has unstaged peak heat above 339. If the crew wants both, the honest move is 2.3.1's own structure — let staging change drag area and shield exposure independently, rather than tuning one number to satisfy both.

Spec audit — FAIL

The catalog recomputes exactly, the ablation curve reproduces its own coefficients, and the claimed skim multiplier now matches the flown high-band array to the digit — but the shipping slice is unflyable: the simulator crashed all three ascents on this flight config, so neither band the slice needs is actually reachable.

slice_bands_reachable — GDD §4.1
Every band the shipping slice needs — suborbital and low — is reachable, and the catalog populates both with at least one fragile piece and at least two size classes.
Found: The catalog half holds: suborbital carries 2 fragile pieces (cracked_camera_dome, battered_antenna_whip) across small/medium/oversized, and low carries 3 (cracked_solar_array, parabolic_dish_section, star_tracker_head) across small/medium/oversized, so crushing and fragility are both met inside the slice. The flight half does not. The simulator flew all three ascents and returned reached=false, why='crashed on ascent', fuel_remaining_kg 0, fuel_margin_pct 0 at 102500 m, 170000 m and 275000 m alike. Zero of the two bands the slice requires are reachable on flight {dry_mass_kg 1200, fuel_capacity_kg 620, thrust_n 16000, fuel_burn_kgs 4}, against a params note claiming 1666 m/s of delta-v and ~315 m/s of spare after a ~1350 m/s suborbital circularisation. Every descent, ablation and economy figure in this audit is therefore priced for orbits the ship cannot currently reach.
Observations — pass the spec, worth a human's eye
  • The ablation cost_curve is internally faithful but roughly five times the flown cost. The high band's claimed optimum is 36.54% of plate; the simulator's cheapest high-band descents burn 3.2% (empty), 5.2% (half) and 7.7% (full hold). Re-plating is billed per percent ablated, so the entire cost-of-flying-hot side of the economy is priced off a number the flight model does not reproduce. A human should decide which of the two is the game before any of these credits are trusted.
  • The altitude risk curve runs backwards in flight. Cheapest ablation by band at equal load is 4.1 / 3.6 / 3.2 empty and 10.0 / 8.7 / 7.7 at full hold, and peak heat is 98 / 93 / 89 at full hold — returning from the high band is 23% cheaper in plate and 9 points cooler than returning from suborbital. The cost_curve array says the opposite, and the audit check is stated against the array, so this passes on paper. The greed axis still works (full hold costs 2.4x empty at high), but §2.3.7's 'the value curve and the risk curve rise together' is not true of the flown numbers.
  • The descent decision the design is built around is being made by a parameter the config does not expose. Two high-band skims remove 5.6% of peak heat (157.1 -> 148.4) for 47 m/s of commit burn and 1.8 extra hours of orbit; changing entry depth from 0 to 39000 m removes 36% (157.1 -> 100.6) for free. Skimming is real and correctly priced, but it is a rounding error next to a lever no one is tuning.
  • Nothing in the shop is forced by survival at base gear. All nine flown descents land soft (2.96 / 3.54 / 4.04 m/s, identical across bands) with plate_survives true and peak heat under reentry.heat_capacity 100 in every case. The params argue that the low band needs heat_shield t1 and the high band t2; the flight model gates neither. Heat shield and parachute currently read as optional comfort rather than the altitude gate §2.3.7 describes.
  • The unstaged shallow braking pass — the GDD's coarse-braking phase, meant to happen while thrust remains — peaks at 407 against a capacity of 100 in the suborbital band, the band the player starts in. The intended descent shape is unavailable exactly where it is supposed to be learned.
  • One skim_heat_multiplier array cannot describe all three bands. The adopted high-band values sit within 0.051 of the flown low band but 0.214 from the flown suborbital band at index 2 (0.944 against 0.730), so suborbital skimming is priced at roughly a quarter of its measured effect. This is a schema shape problem, not a tuning choice: the field would have to become per-band for the Balancer to fix it.
  • landing.parachute_area_m2 is absent from the params, so the simulator solved for the 610.5 m2 that reproduces descent_speed_full_hold_ms 4.18. The parachute check therefore partly measures the claim rather than testing it. State the area and the measurement becomes independent.
  • Steady-state towing has a 6.8x margin against shear, which is comfortable, but latch shear at latch_jerk_coefficient 1.0 fires above about 1.8 m/s closing speed on a 500 kg low-band piece. That is a narrow approach window for the band the slice ships with, and it is the first thing worth feeling in the phase 3a tether demo before hand_magnet t2 is priced as the answer to it.
Catalog concerns raised by the Economy Balancer

Not audit findings. These are problems the Balancer hit while pricing the catalog and could not fix, because it does not own that data. On a failing run they are routed to the Debris Designer; on a passing run they land here.

  • dense_avionics_core, iridium_battery_stack and gold_foil_bus_module, property mass_kg: the high band's hand-tetherable mediums plateau at 980-1000 kg, so a legal two-tether high-band load is 2000 kg and base-tier EVA acceleration falls to 280/2110 = 0.13 m/s^2 — below the 'noticeably heavier but still controllable' target the charter sets, and half the 0.25 m/s^2 the low band gives. The Debris Designer already routed this to the Jetpack upgrade as the counterweight, and I agree that is the right lever, but Jetpack is not among the shipping slice's six upgrade parts, so no tier of it exists for me to price. This does not bind the slice (the high band is deferred with it) and I have balanced against the catalog as it stands; flagging it so the finding is on record before the high band comes online.
