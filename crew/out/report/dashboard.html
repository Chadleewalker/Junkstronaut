The ablation curve

curve ok, requirement failsCheapest descent is 0 skims from the lower band, 1 skim from the mid band, 1 skim from the upper band. A single-pass optimum here is expected and is not a defect — the plunge is always the cheapest way down, and the design does not try to out-price it. What fails is the separate rule that a heavy haul from high up must be UNABLE to plunge — see the audit. That is a question about survivable peak heat, not about cost, and this chart is not evidence either way.

What am I looking at?

The hardest constraint in the document (§2.3.1). One screaming plunge is expensive; a dozen feather-light passes are expensive; the cheapest descent should be a planned 2–4 committed passes. Each ring marks where that band's curve bottoms out. These are the Balancer's own numbers plotted verbatim, not the renderer's arithmetic — so the curve and the audit beneath it can never disagree.

The ablation surface

read the valleyThe dark line along the floor is the cheapest strategy at every entry speed. It reports where the model bottoms out; here it sits at 1 from the upper band.

What am I looking at?

Plate burned as a function of both inputs at once — how fast you are coming in, and how many passes you split it into. Two independent variables and one output, so this is the one place a surface beats a line chart.

The dark line is the valley floor — the cheapest pass count at every entry speed. It is the answer to the hardest question in §2.3.1, and it should stay inside 2–4 across the whole span. Where it climbs toward the back, higher altitudes are demanding more passes; where it runs flat, a single strategy covers every altitude.

What am I looking at?

5184 worlds, varying planet radius, gravity, air density, ship frontal area, dry mass, tank size and engine. Each scored against 9 targets taken from the design document. Playtest — targets missed

targets missed8 findings, 1 blocking, 6 proposed value changes.

What am I looking at?

The config flies: the base ship survives its own launch, both routes reach the band, and the module can only come home aerobraked — but the capacity window that carries the endgame is 2.7% wide, the module needs two skims from the top rather than one, the full hold is 1.23x the ship's mass instead of the promised 2x, and no measured configuration anywhere in the grid survives an unstaged braking pass.

  • blocking capacity window is 2 7 percent wide (§2.2, tuning) — Flown at the commit floor the window is (217.8, 223.7) — 2.7% wide. The module's best skimmed peak from the top is 217.8 and its bottom-sample plunge is 223.7, and the whole shield ladder (219 / 221 / 223) is squeezed inside those two numbers. The full hold's plunge is not the lower bound at all: it peaks at 104.3 / 128.6 / 163.4, less than half the module's, so ordinary cargo is nowhere near the constraint. The balancer's own note records the window closing to 0.8% at dry mass 900 and going negative at 1,000, so the mechanic survives on a 100 kg margin in a parameter nobody is guarding.
  • significant module needs two skims from the top (§2.6, design) — From the top sample the module peaks at 335.5 on a plunge, 243.2 with one skim and 217.8 with two. 243.2 is above every purchasable capacity (219 / 221 / 223), so one skim does not bring it home from where it actually hangs; two does. From the middle the same pattern holds — 268.5 / 224.4 / 213.9, with the one-skim figure again above the ladder. Only from the bottom sample does a single skim work (208.2). The finale is a three-cycle descent, not a two-cycle one.
  • significant unstaged pass survivable nowhere in the grid (§2.2, design) — The exploration grid satisfies unstaged_pass_survivable in 8.0% of 5,184 configurations — the lowest rate of any target, and not one of the twelve best-scoring worlds satisfies it (every top entry reports false, with unstaged peaks of 247, 311 and 376 against capacities in the low hundreds). The verification sweep does report the crew's own shallow unstaged pass surviving at 71 and 78 for bottom and middle, and no row at all for the top — so the choice exists in shallow passes low in the band and vanishes exactly where the endgame is flown. This is the rule, not the numbers: 92% of the space cannot buy it.
  • significant full hold is 1 23x not 2x (§2.5, tuning) — Six slots at the catalog's 30.43 kg/slot is a 182.6 kg hold on an 800 kg ship — 1.23x. The consequences are all measured, not argued: the full hold lands at 3.22 m/s where an empty ship lands at 2.91, a 0.31 m/s spread across the entire greed decision; storage tier 2 moves ten slots' touchdown to 3.42 m/s, still soft on the base canopy with no purchase required; and the full hold's plunge peaks at 163.4 from the top against the empty ship's 147.8. Satisfying the doubling from dry mass needs 183 kg, a staged ballistic coefficient of 37 — under the ~50 floor where first air contact erases the orbit and the skim mechanic ceases to exist. The catalog is the only lever that closes it.
  • minor one pass is cheapest everywhere confirmed (§2.2, design) — Confirmed in all twelve descent rows: cheapest_pass_count is 1 everywhere, from 7.3% of plate (top, empty) to 40.9% (bottom, endgame haul), and cost rises monotonically with pass count in every row — the bottom full hold runs 8.8 / 15.2 / 22.1 / 29.9 / 38.6 / 72.2 / 253.7 across 1 through 16 passes. The grid agrees that the benefit saturates: skim_benefit_saturates holds in 88.9% of configurations, the highest rate of any target, and every measured multiplier array is flat after index 1 or 2. The design's own withdrawal of the cost target is the correct call and should not be reopened.
  • significant middle sample circularises on fumes (§2.5, tuning) — The base ship circularises at the bottom sample with 6.9% of tank (109.83 kg) and at the middle with 2.9% (46.36 kg), and cannot circularise at the top at all ('out of fuel before circularising', 19.2% left on the arc). The middle sample's 2.9% is a circularisation the sweep scores as reached but which leaves no deorbit budget — a route the player can take that strands them, one band above where the design says it should already be impossible. The arc margins are healthy throughout: 29.4% / 23.8% / 18.0%, with EVA windows of 125.9 / 202.1 / 368.3 s, so the arc is genuinely the first launch's route as designed and GDD 4.5's 97-second question is answered generously at 125.9 s.
  • minor parachute prediction now holds (§2.3, tuning) — With area 630 m2 at Cd 1.8 stated in the params, the closed form predicts 3.23 m/s and the integrator lands the full hold at 3.22 m/s at all three sample altitudes — delta 0.01 m/s, spread across bands 0.00, independent true. The tautology is genuinely gone. But the predicted number is 3.22, not the GDD's 4.4, because the mass under the canopy is 982.6 kg rather than the ~2x hold the document assumes — the same shortfall as the doubling finding, seen through the chute. The module lands at 6.83 m/s on the base canopy and needs parachute tier 2 (1,250 m2, 4.84 m/s) for a soft touchdown, so the Parachute track is what buys a pristine delivery.
  • minor fuel margin sane is rare in the grid (§4.4, design) — fuel_margin_sane holds in 18.7% of 5,184 grid configurations and launch_survives_itself in 34.0% — the second and third rarest targets after unstaged_pass_survivable at 8.0%. The crew's config hits both, and the climb peaks at 50.0 / 47.5 / 46.7 bar units against capacity 219, reproducing GDD 2.2's stated 47.5 exactly at the middle sample. That is a real achievement on a thin part of the space, and it is also a warning: the locked planet sits somewhere only a fifth of worlds reach, so any future change to radius, gravity or sea-level density is very likely to break the ascent rather than adjust it.

What the simulator cannot tell you
  • The simulator is a point mass with a perfect pilot. Every skim altitude in the committed_descents table was found by scanning — the module's two-skim survival from the top rests on hitting 24,585 m, and a human flying by the CHUTE lamp and an aim indicator has no instrument that reads that number. The 2.7% capacity window is measured, but its playable width is smaller than its measured width by an amount only a human can determine.
  • The unstaged_braking table has rows for bottom and middle only — no top-sample row was flown. I read the missing row as unflown rather than as a failure, so the claim that the unstaged choice vanishes at the top of the band is inference from the grid's 8.0% satisfaction rate, not a direct measurement of this config.
  • The 133 kg/slot proposal is arithmetic on the catalog's own mean, not a flown result. It closes the doubling and the canopy margin simultaneously in closed form; whether the resulting value economy still clears the shop in 12-15 runs has not been simulated, and the balancer's pricing notes would all need recomputing against it.
  • The heat capacity ladder proposal (210 / 215 / 220) is derived from the same committed_descents peaks the current ladder was fitted to, so it inherits whatever bias those measurements carry. If the module's 217.8 two-skim peak moves under a different scan resolution, the proposed ladder breaks in the same direction the current one does — it is a wider spacing of the same fragile window, not an escape from it.
  • I did not evaluate whether one skim versus two changes how the finale feels. The measured difference is roughly six minutes of wall clock per pass at warp and one more heat cycle at an escalating toll; whether a three-cycle finale reads as a climactic sequence or as a chore is exactly the judgement the charter says belongs to a human.

Spec audit — FAIL

3 failed18 of 21 rules hold. Failing: full_hold_doubles_mass, heavy_descent_requires_multi_pass, parachute_speed_under_soft_threshold.

What am I looking at?

The flight, ablation and economy numbers hold up — the skim multiplier is now the flown one and the parachute claim is finally independent — but the module cannot come home on two passes from where it hangs (243.2 against a 223 ceiling, and no capacity closes that gap), and the shipping slice is too light to load either the ship or the canopy: a full hold is 1.23x, not 2x, and lands at 3.22 m/s.

full_hold_doubles_mass — GDD §2.5
A full hold roughly doubles the rocket's mass at starting storage size.
Found: Recomputed independently from the catalog: spawn-weighted over the shipping slice, hand-tetherable only, at compactor_tier 1, mass*weight sums to 8,126 kg over 267 weighted slots = 30.43 kg/slot. 6 base slots x 30.43 = 182.6 kg against dry_mass_kg 800 = 1.23x. Read the other way the GDD 4.3 allows — one slot per piece — the spawn-weighted mean piece is 8,126/155 = 52.4 kg, so 6 slots = 314.5 kg and 1.39x. Both are outside the 1.5-2.5x that 'roughly doubles' permits, low by a factor of 1.63 on the first reading. Dry mass cannot absorb it: 183 kg dry gives a staged ballistic coefficient of 183/(1.4*3.5) = 37, under the ~50 floor at which the atmosphere stops the ship instead of braking it, which deletes the skim mechanic the endgame rests on. The catalog's shipping-slice masses are the only lever left.
heavy_descent_requires_multi_pass — GDD §2.2
The endgame haul cannot stay under the bar on a single pass at any entry depth, can on two, while an empty ship and a full hold still can on one from anywhere in the band.
Found: commit_floor_m is 8,000 m — 35,000 m below atmosphere_top_m 43,000 — so the floor genuinely constrains the entry and the check is live. Judged on committed_descents only. Part 1 holds: the endgame haul's plunge peaks 223.7 / 268.5 / 335.5 at bottom / middle / top, all above heat_capacity 219 and above every purchasable tier (221, 223). Part 3 holds: empty 93.8 / 116.0 / 147.8 and full hold 104.3 / 128.6 / 163.4, all under 219 from anywhere, so ordinary cargo is never forced to aerobrake. Part 2 fails: one skim (two passes) brings the module to 208.2 from the bottom but only 224.4 from the middle and 243.2 from the top — both above the 223 ceiling — and the module hangs at 277,000 m, above the top sample. It needs two skims, three heat cycles, to reach 213.9 / 217.8. No heat_capacity fixes this: satisfying part 1 requires C < 223.7 (the bottom plunge) and satisfying part 2 requires C > 243.2 (the top one-skim), and 243.2 > 223.7, so the two bounds are contradictory. The rule as written cannot be satisfied by any capacity at this dry mass and drag configuration.
parachute_speed_under_soft_threshold — GDD §2.3
Full-hold parachute descent speed is under the 5 m/s soft-landing line but above about 3.5 m/s, so the Parachute upgrade is a real purchase.
Found: v = sqrt(2mg / (rho0 * Cd * A)) = sqrt(2 * 982.6 * 9.0 / (1.5 * 1.8 * 630)) = sqrt(17,686.8/1,701) = 3.22 m/s, and the integrator lands it at 3.22 at all three sample altitudes. That is 0.28 m/s under the 3.5 floor — soft with no purchase and no margin to sell. The cause is the mass under the canopy, not the canopy: at 630 m2 and Cd 1.8, reaching 3.5 m/s needs 3.5^2 * 94.5 = 1,157.6 kg loaded, i.e. 357.6 kg of cargo across 6 slots, 59.6 kg/slot against the catalog's 30.43. The canopy itself cannot be the lever — 630 m2 and Cd 1.8 are GDD 2.3 constants and shrinking them is the tautology that rule exists to forbid.
Observations — pass the spec, worth a human's eye
  • The heat-shield track buys almost nothing. 219 -> 221 -> 223 is +0.9% per tier for 700 and 1,600 credits — 4 bar units total across the shop's most expensive progression line. GDD 2.5 also makes the shield the only mass-reducing purchase, the counterweight to storage-driven landing difficulty, and dry_mass_kg stays 800 at every tier. A player who buys both tiers will not feel either.
  • The module's entire survivable window is (217.8, 223.7) — 2.7% wide, with the whole capacity ladder squeezed inside it. The Balancer records the window closing to 0.8% at dry mass 900 and going negative at 1,000. The endgame therefore rests on a ~100 kg margin in a parameter no rule guards; anything that touches dry mass, shield drag or reference area will delete the win condition silently.
  • Storage is only half double-edged. Tier 2 costs 1,500, raises the profit ceiling 67%, and moves full-hold touchdown from 3.22 to 3.42 m/s — both soft on the base canopy with no compensating purchase. GDD 2.5's 'raises the difficulty of the flight home' is not in these numbers, and the spread across the whole greed decision (empty 2.91 to full 3.22 m/s) is 0.31 m/s.
  • The base ship can circularise at the middle sample with 2.9% of tank — 46.4 kg — which the sweep scores as reached but which leaves nothing to deorbit on. That is a route the player can legally take one band above where the design says orbit should already be unaffordable, and it ends in GDD 2.7's stranded state.
  • Value carries no mass term. cracked_deep_space_dish (78 kg, medium, fragile, 217,500 m) prices at 1,154.8 while cracked_command_module_hatch (112 kg, small, fragile, 268,000 m) prices at 40 * 4.7391 * 3 = 568.7 — the heavier, higher piece is worth half the lighter one. GDD 2.6's 'value scales with mass on top of the altitude gradient' is approximated by the catalog's masses rising with altitude, but the coupling itself is absent from economy.
  • The staged-versus-unstaged braking choice is unverified where it matters. unstaged_braking carries rows for bottom (71) and middle (78) and none for the top, and the exploration grid satisfies unstaged survivability in 8.0% of configurations. The choice GDD 2.2 describes may simply not exist at the altitude the finale is flown from.
  • One arithmetic slip in balance_notes worth correcting even though nothing fails on it: the module's steady tow tension is quoted as 388.2 N, but the note's own formula gives 520 * 3,600 / (110 + 3,600) = 504.6 N. Still 1.78x under the 900 N hold force, so the rule holds either way.
Catalog concerns raised by the Economy Balancer

Not audit findings. These are problems the Balancer hit while pricing the catalog and could not fix, because it does not own that data. On a failing run they are routed to the Debris Designer; on a passing run they land here.

  • The shipping slice's mass per slot, against GDD 2.5's full-hold doubling and GDD 2.3's parachute margin at the same time. Spawn-weighted over the bottom two thirds, hand-tetherable only, at compactor_tier 1, the catalog prices out at 30.43 kg per slot, so six base slots is a 182.6 kg hold. For GDD 2.5's 'a full hold roughly doubles the rocket's mass' to be true, dry_mass_kg would have to be 183 — a staged ballistic coefficient of 37, below the ~50 cliff where the atmosphere stops the ship instead of braking it, which deletes the skim mechanic and crashes the ascent. I balanced against the catalog as it stands and left the rule broken at 1.23x rather than satisfy it from dry mass. The same shortfall costs the parachute its headroom: at 630 m2 and Cd 1.8 the full hold lands at 3.23 m/s, 0.28 m/s under the ~3.5 the audit wants, because there is not enough cargo mass to load the canopy. Both close at once if the slice averages about 133 kg per slot — a full hold would then be 800 kg on an 800 kg ship, exactly 2.00x, and land at 4.11 m/s, in the middle of the soft band. That is a 4.4x raise on the pieces below 203,333 m and it is a much bigger ask than the 2.2x of the last revision; the honest alternative, if the floor cannot carry that, is for the GDD to withdraw the doubling sentence, because no dry mass satisfies it and the flight at once.
  • Storage tier 2 has no effect on landing difficulty, which is the pair GDD 2.5 designs around. At 30.43 kg per slot, going from 6 slots to 10 adds 121.7 kg to a 800 kg ship and moves the full-hold touchdown from 3.23 to 3.42 m/s — both soft, on the base canopy, with no purchase required. GDD 2.5's 'storage raises the profit ceiling and the difficulty of the flight home' is currently only the first half: the profit ceiling rises 67%, the difficulty does not move. This is the same per-slot mass finding as above seen from the shop's side, and it is why Armstrong's module rather than a maxed hold is doing all of the Parachute track's work.
  • armstrongs_module at 3,600 kg against the heaviest ordinary piece, recorded as a property to protect rather than a defect. severed_boom_mast at 165 kg is 21.8x lighter and impact_pitted_engine_bell at 122 kg is the heaviest the magnet can legally take, so the module is 4.5x a maxed ten-slot hold on its own. That ratio is what keeps every ordinary haul under 164 on the bar while the module plunges at 223.7-335.5, and it is the only reason a capacity window exists at all — at drag_coefficient_shield 1.4 that window is (217.8, 223.7), 2.7% wide, and it closes from below the moment the top of the catalog drifts toward a full hold's mass. If a future pass rescales masses, the quantity to hold is module_mass / (base_slots * shipping_slice_mean), currently 19.7.
