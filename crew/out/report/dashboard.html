Value costs mass

The design's central bet (§2.3.7): the better the haul, the harder the ride home. If this cloud trends up and to the right, the bet holds — the valuable pieces are the heavy ones. A flat cloud means upgrades will outrun difficulty.

The ablation curve

The hardest constraint in the document (§2.3.1). One screaming plunge is expensive; a dozen feather-light passes are expensive; the cheapest descent should be a planned 2–4 committed passes. Each ring marks where that band's curve bottoms out.

  • Suborbital — cheapest at 0 skims as designed
  • Low orbit — cheapest at 1 skim as designed
  • High orbit — cheapest at 2 skims as designed

Is any size class strictly best?

Value per cargo slot. If one class towers over the others at every band, the greed decision collapses — the player just learns the rule and stops choosing.

The ablation surface

Plate burned as a function of both inputs at once — how fast you are coming in, and how many passes you split it into. Two independent variables and one output, so this is the one place a surface beats a line chart.

The dark line is the valley floor — the cheapest pass count at every entry speed. It is the answer to the hardest question in §2.3.1, and it should stay inside 2–4 across the whole span. Where it climbs toward the back, higher bands are demanding more passes; where it runs flat, a single strategy covers every altitude.

Where the design's targets are reachable at all

2916 worlds, varying gravity, air density, atmosphere thickness, ship frontal area and dry mass. Each scored against seven targets taken from the design document. A short bar means almost no configuration anywhere satisfies that target — which is a fact about the rule, not about the current numbers.

Best world found: 7/8 targets — gravity 9.81 m/s2, air density 1.2 kg/m3, scale height 1680 m, frontal area 12 m2, dry mass 800 kg (ballistic coefficient 44.4 kg/m2, full hold touching down at 4.75 m/s).

Playtest — targets missed

Flown, this config lands softly from every band at every load and never burns through its plate, but the two-to-four-pass ablation optimum the design is built around does not exist — a single committed plunge is the cheapest descent in all nine band/load cases, and the grid says that is a design problem, not a tuning one.

  • blocking ablation optimum is always one pass (§2.3.1, design) — cheapest_pass_count is 1 in all nine descents. The curve is monotonically increasing everywhere: high band full hold 13.6 -> 18.7 -> 19.5 -> 19.7 -> 20.3 ...; low band full hold 13.1 -> 17.2 -> 18.1 -> 18.5; suborbital empty 5.2 -> 6.0 -> 6.3 -> 6.5. The first extra pass costs 4.1-5.1 plate-percent against a claimed saving of 0.15-0.92. No U-curve exists at any band or load.
  • blocking skimming barely cools the committed entry (§2.3.1, design) — Measured multipliers at the shallowest entry depth are suborbital [1, 0.951, 0.897, 0.684], low [1, 0.977, 0.955, 0.934], high [1, 0.984, 0.968, 0.954] — so the params' array is right for high, pessimistic for low, and badly pessimistic for suborbital. But the effect is dwarfed by entry depth: on the high band, going from entry_depth 0 to 36000 m drops entry peak heat 163.1 -> 93.8 (a 0.575x multiplier) while three skims only reach 0.954. Depth is roughly 8x the lever that skimming is, and each skim costs 43-54 m/s of commit dv and about an hour of flight. In the grid, skimming_cools_the_entry is satisfied by 44.1% of configurations, but every config that satisfies it strongly does so with a low ballistic coefficient (the 0.363 and 0.211 multiplier rows sit at beta 44.4 and 133.3) and those same configs fail unstaged_pass_survivable with peak heats of 371-523.
  • blocking beta and unstaged survival are mutually exclusive (§2.3.1, design) — unstaged_pass_survivable is the rarest target in the whole grid at 15.4%, and fuel_margin_sane is next at 16.2%. Across the 12 top-scoring configs, every one that measures a strong skim effect (multipliers 0.211-0.363) records unstaged_peak_heat of 355-523, and every one that survives unstaged (peak heat 47-90) measures skim multipliers of 0.945-0.987 — i.e. skimming that does nothing. No configuration in 2916 scores 8 of 8. The current config sits on the survivable side: unstaged shallow passes peak at 62 (suborbital) and 71 (low), both safe, which is why its skims are near-inert.
  • significant peak heat exceeds the bar but nothing burns (§2.3.1, tuning) — Sixteen entries in the descent table peak above 100 — high band full hold reaches 102 at 10, 14 and 15 passes; low band full hold reaches 101 from 11 passes up; suborbital full hold reaches 101 from 6 passes. Yet every descent reports plate_survives true and soft_landing true, and the skim table shows entry peaks of 131.5 (low, direct) and 163.1 (high, direct) with touchdown still 3.25 m/s and soft_landing true. Either the 100 bar does not gate cargo damage in the model or the two heat scales are not the same quantity; as reported, the ship exceeds its stated heat capacity by 63% and suffers nothing.
  • significant difficulty does not rise with band (§2.3.7, tuning) — At the cheapest (one-pass) descent, ablation is 5.2 / 4.4 / 4.5 percent empty and 15.6 / 13.1 / 13.6 percent full for suborbital / low / high. The high band is cheaper to come home from than suborbital at every load, and the ordering across bands is essentially flat — a 1.06x spread against the claimed 1.43x. Cargo mass, by contrast, does move it: full hold costs 3x an empty hold (13.6 vs 4.5 on the high band). Mass carries the difficulty curve; altitude does not.
  • minor full hold lands soft everywhere with no margin pressure (§2.3.2, tuning) — Confirmed exactly: touchdown is 3.25 m/s empty, 3.99 half hold, 4.60 full hold, identical across all three bands, and soft_landing is true in all nine. The sqrt(mass) law holds — 4.6/sqrt(2) = 3.25. But because a base hold is the largest load the sim flies, no measured case ever crosses 5.0, so the Parachute upgrade has no measured case in which it is needed; the crossing the params describe (a 1665 kg haul at 5.49 m/s) is asserted, not flown. The parachute area was also not in the params — the sim solved for 630.1 m2 from the 4.6 m/s claim, so this is a consistency check, not an independent measurement.
  • minor ascent margins are thin and inverted against the upgrade tree (§2.3.6, tuning) — All three bands are reachable at base config, including the deferred high band: fuel margins 16.6% / 13.5% / 11.8% (316.19 / 255.75 / 223.80 kg remaining). That settles the open question the economy notes could not — the tank is oversized for its stated job, since the semester slice only needs two bands. It also leaves fuel_tank tier 1 (450 credits) buying nothing the player needs, and 11.8% is a thin reserve for the return burn the sim does not charge for. In the grid, fuel_margin_sane holds in only 16.2% of worlds, so a thin margin here is normal for the space rather than a mistake.

What the simulator cannot tell you
  • The heat scale is normalised so that an empty single-pass suborbital descent peaks at 100, per the sweep's own calibration note. Every heat figure I quote is relative to that anchor, so 'peak 163 on the high band' means 1.63x that reference descent, not 163 physical units of anything. The finding that peaks exceed heat_capacity 100 without consequence follows from comparing two scales that may not be the same quantity — that is the first thing to check before acting on it.
  • The parachute area was inferred by the simulator, not read from the params. Every touchdown speed in the sweep is therefore anchored to the 4.6 m/s claim it was solved from, which is why 'full hold lands soft' is a consistency check rather than independent evidence. Adding landing.parachute_area_m2 to the params would make the next sweep's touchdown numbers mean something on their own.
  • The sim flies a point mass with a perfect pilot: no attitude error, so off_retrograde_penalty is never exercised; no debris collisions; no cargo damage accumulation modelled during the pass. The claim that plate_survives in every descent should be read as 'the ablation arithmetic stays under the plate', not as 'a human can fly this'.
  • The exploration grid varies world and ship geometry but not the ablation knobs themselves (cycle_toll_base_pct, heat_cost_coefficient, skim_peak). So its 44.1% satisfaction rate for skimming_cools_the_entry measures whether the physics can produce a cooling effect, not whether the pricing model can turn that effect into a U-curve. My design-versus-tuning call on the ablation optimum rests on the physics half of that; a sweep over the three ablation knobs at fixed world would settle the other half and is the single most useful thing the next run could add.
  • No skim series in the verification sweep reaches a periapsis of 40-45 km, where the Researcher's own dv table says a pass is worth 25-85 m/s rather than a feather. The deepest skims flown are shallow enough that skim_peak_heat never exceeds 9.6. If the crew wants the aerobrake mechanic to survive, that unflown depth band is where to look before adopting the flat-toll fallback — my 'design' verdict on the U-curve is calibrated to the depths actually flown.
  • The descent table's pass_counts_reachable has gaps (the high band empty case skips 13, 15, 16) and ablation is not monotonic in a few rows — high band empty dips from 8.3 at 11 passes to 8.1 at 12, and high band half hold from 14.4 at 8 to 13.3 at 9. Those non-monotonicities are larger than the 0.15-percent differences the params' U-curve depends on, which is independent evidence that the intended optimum is below the model's own noise floor.

Spec audit — FAIL

Everything checks out except the one rule the whole reentry design rests on: the flown physics makes a single committed plunge the cheapest descent at every band and every load, so the 2-4 pass optimum the params assert does not exist in the game the params describe.

cheapest_descent_is_one_or_two_skims — GDD §2.3.1
The cheapest descent is 1 to 2 skims from the high band; no band's optimum exceeds the high band's; each minimum equals its optimal_skims entry; and the curve is what the stated model actually produces.
Found: The array and the model agree with each other and disagree with the flown game. Model check (coefficient 0.000018, exponent 3, tolls 0.16 x 1.35^(i-1), skim_peak 29 costing 0.4390 each), recomputed at four points per band: high k=0 = 0.000018 x 119^3 + 0.16 = 30.333 + 0.16 = 30.49; high k=2 = 0.000018 x 115.906^3 + (0.16+0.216+0.2916) + 2 x 0.4390 = 28.028 + 0.668 + 0.878 = 29.57; low k=1 = 0.000018 x 98.4^3 + 0.376 + 0.439 = 17.150 + 0.815 = 17.96; low k=3 = 16.528 + 1.061 + 1.317 = 18.91; suborbital k=0 = 10.292 + 0.16 = 10.45 and k=2 = 9.510 + 0.668 + 0.878 = 11.06. Every recomputed point matches cost_curve to within 0.03%. Minima sit at index 2 (high, 29.57), index 1 (low, 17.96) and index 0 (suborbital, 10.45), matching optimal_skims 2/1/0, and no band's optimum exceeds the high band's (10.45 and 17.96 both < 29.57). But the flown descents put cheapest_pass_count at 1 in all nine cases, with ablation strictly increasing in pass count everywhere: high full hold 13.6 -> 18.7 -> 19.5 -> 19.7; low full hold 13.1 -> 17.2 -> 18.1 -> 18.5; suborbital empty 5.2 -> 6.0 -> 6.3 -> 6.5. The first extra pass costs 4.1-5.1 plate-percent against the model's claimed saving of 0.15-0.92. GDD 2.3.1 requires the cheapest descent to be 2-4 committed passes and 'never a single plunge'; the game as parameterised makes the single plunge cheapest at every band and every load, and the params' own suborbital optimum of 0 skims (1 pass) already concedes that below 2. A curve that reproduces its own coefficients faithfully still fails when the coefficients price a cooling effect the simulator measured not to exist at the depths flown.
Observations — pass the spec, worth a human's eye
  • The skim economy is priced band-blind. One skim_heat_multiplier array [1, 0.984, 0.974, 0.972] is applied to all three bands, but the simulator measured suborbital at [1, 0.951, 0.897, 0.684] — index 3 is 0.288 away, so a third suborbital skim actually cools the entry four times as much as the params believe. The high band matches to 0.018 and the low band to 0.038. The single array passes the audit on the band it was checked against and is meaningfully wrong on the band a new player flies first.
  • Even if the U-curve worked as authored it would be invisible. The high band's optimum beats a single plunge by 0.92 plate-percent and beats one skim by 0.15 — under half a credit of re-plating at replate_cost_per_pct 3. A mechanic the player cannot feel is not a decision, and 4.5 already names the flat-toll-with-a-cap fallback.
  • Skimming is the wrong lever to be tuning. On the high band, moving entry depth from 0 to 36000 m drops entry peak heat 163.1 -> 93.8 (0.575x) while three skims reach only 0.954 — depth is roughly eight times the lever skimming is. And each skim costs 43-54 m/s of commit dv and about an hour of simulated flight (high band: 1.25 h direct, 4.18 h with three skims). Asking a player to spend an hour of time warp to save under one plate-percent is a worse trade than the arithmetic alone suggests.
  • The 100-heat bar appears to be decorative. Sixteen entries in the descent table peak above heat_capacity 100, and the skim table records direct-entry peaks of 131.5 (low) and 163.1 (high) — 63% over the stated capacity — yet every case reports plate_survives true, soft_landing true and touchdown 3.25 m/s. Either the bar does not gate cargo damage in the model or the two heat figures are different quantities wearing the same name. Worth resolving before the Coder implements 2.3.1's 'cargo damage begins at 100% heat'.
  • The difficulty curve is carried entirely by cargo, not by altitude. One-pass ablation runs 5.2 / 4.4 / 4.5 percent empty and 15.6 / 13.1 / 13.6 percent full for suborbital / low / high — the high band is cheaper to come home from than the suborbital band at every load, against heat_index's claimed 83/100/119. A full hold costs 3x an empty one. 4.5's risk 2 is about the curve inverting with upgrades; this is the curve already flat with respect to the axis 2.3.7 says should drive it.
  • reentry.off_retrograde_penalty is 1, i.e. no penalty at all. 2.3.1 asks for heat to build faster with the cosine of off-retrograde drift, which is what makes holding attitude a skill during the plasma phase. As set, the player can tumble through the entry at no cost.
  • All three bands are reachable at base config, including the deferred high band (fuel margins 16.6% / 13.5% / 11.8%). The base tank is therefore oversized for the two-band slice it was sized against, and fuel_tank tier 1 at 450 credits buys nothing the player needs — the first of twelve purchases is a dead purchase. The Balancer flagged that it could not settle this without a flown ascent; it is now settled.
  • The parachute rule is checked against itself. parachute_area_m2 is absent from the params, so the simulator solved 630.1 m2 from descent_speed_full_hold_ms 4.6 and then measured 4.6. The number the check depends on has never been independently flown, and because a base hold is the heaviest load simulated, no case crosses 5.0 — the greedy-haul crossing the Balancer describes (1665 kg at 5.49 m/s), which is the whole justification for the Parachute upgrade, is asserted arithmetic rather than a flight. Adding parachute_area_m2 to the params would make it measurable.
