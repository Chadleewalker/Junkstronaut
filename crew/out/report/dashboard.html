Value costs mass

The design's central bet (§2.3.7): the better the haul, the harder the ride home. If this cloud trends up and to the right, the bet holds — the valuable pieces are the heavy ones. A flat cloud means upgrades will outrun difficulty.

The ablation curve

The hardest constraint in the document (§2.3.1). One screaming plunge is expensive; a dozen feather-light passes are expensive; the cheapest descent should be a planned 2–4 committed passes. Each ring marks where that band's curve bottoms out.

Is any size class strictly best?

Value per cargo slot. If one class towers over the others at every band, the greed decision collapses — the player just learns the rule and stops choosing.

The ablation surface

Plate burned as a function of both inputs at once — how fast you are coming in, and how many passes you split it into. Two independent variables and one output, so this is the one place a surface beats a line chart.

The dark line is the valley floor — the cheapest pass count at every entry speed. It is the answer to the hardest question in §2.3.1, and it should stay inside 2–4 across the whole span. Where it climbs toward the back, higher bands are demanding more passes; where it runs flat, a single strategy covers every altitude.

Where the design's targets are reachable at all

324 worlds, varying gravity, air density, atmosphere thickness, ship frontal area and dry mass. Each scored against seven targets taken from the design document. A short bar means almost no configuration anywhere satisfies that target — which is a fact about the rule, not about the current numbers.

Best world found: 7/8 targets — gravity 10 m/s2, air density 0.02 kg/m3, scale height 7.2 m, frontal area 3.5 m2, dry mass 180 kg (ballistic coefficient 35.5 kg/m2, cheapest descent 1 passes).

Playtest — targets missed

Flown, this configuration never lands: 9 of 9 descents touch down at 21–23 m/s with the parachute never deploying, and the 2–4 pass ablation optimum is unreachable — one pass is cheapest in every single descent and only 3.1% of 324 sampled worlds hit the window at all.

  • blocking soft landing never occurs (§2.3.2, tuning) — 0 of 9 descents landed soft. Touchdown was 21.03–23.34 m/s vertical across every band and every load (suborbital empty 22.39, suborbital full 21.03, low full 22.35, high empty 23.34, high full 22.25) and the parachute deployed in none of them. The ship is still carrying its orbital horizontal velocity at ground contact — vertical 21–23 m/s while total speed stays above the 58 m/s plasma/chute gate — so the 4.59 m/s figure is a terminal velocity under a 700 m2 canopy that the flight never reaches. Every downstream rule in 2.3.2 (soft vs hard, no_gear_multiplier 2, fragile_multiplier 2) and the entire Parachute upgrade line (Cd 1.5 → 2.1 → 2.9) are therefore inert as flown. In the exploration grid full_hold_lands_soft holds in 13.6% of 324 worlds and in 12 of the 12 top-scoring ones, at 3.00–4.86 m/s.
  • blocking ablation optimum not reachable (§2.3.1, design) — cheapest_pass_count is 1 in all 9 flown descents, and plate cost is monotone increasing in pass count everywhere: suborbital empty 20.6 / 25.9 / 28.3 / 56.5% at 1 / 2 / 3 / 16 passes; low full hold 50.4 / 57.7 / 61.7 / 64.9 / 67.8 / 70.7% at 1–6; high full hold 49.8% at 1 rising to 91.7% at 9. The mechanism is visible in peak heat: the 1-pass plunge peaks 100 while the shallowest 2-pass descent peaks 106 — inside a 90 m atmosphere with a 5 s heat time constant, a long shallow pass equilibrates and a steep one does not, so shallow is hotter, not cooler. Since cost = toll + C*peak^E with toll > 0 and both peaks above the plunge's, the ordering cannot be inverted by any C or E. The grid agrees: optimum_in_window is satisfied by 3.1% of 324 worlds (~10 configs) and by none of the 12 best-scoring ones, all of which top out at 7/8 with this the single missed target. This is §4.5 risk 1 confirmed, not cleared; the fallback the GDD already names — flat per-pass cost with a hard cap on passes — is the move, and the schema field requiring optimal_pass_count in 2–4 is itself encoding an unreachable rule.
  • significant heat bar saturated on every loaded descent (§2.3.1, tuning) — Peak heat exceeded the 100 cap in 6 of 9 descents — 128 and 150 (suborbital half/full hold), 133 and 154 (low), 133 and 152 (high), reaching 158 on the deeper multi-pass entries — yet all 9 report landed = true and plate_survives = true, because the sweep prices shield plate only and never charges the cargo damage the design specifies. The cause is a calibration collision: the heat scale is normalised so the easiest possible descent (empty, suborbital, single pass) peaks at exactly 100, so the cap equals the best case and every loaded reentry begins over the cargo-damage line. Compounding it, neither heat_shield upgrade tier touches heat_capacity — both move plate_capacity_pct only (100 → 150 → 210) — so GDD 2.3.1's 'heat shield upgrades buy margin' is false for the heat bar; they buy passes, not peak.
  • significant band heat spread is flat in flight (§2.3.7, design) — Empty single-pass peak heat is 100 / 103 / 105 across suborbital / low / high — a 5% spread — and cheapest plate cost is 20.6 / 22.3 / 23.6% — a 15% spread. Neither reaches the claimed 1.27x. baseline.json's own derivation explains it: entry speeds span only 151.1–163.7 m/s because they are trapped between circular and escape speed, and the index is computed from that ratio rather than from anything the ship experiences. The index is now independent of the flight (which was the point of moving it) and independent in the direction of disagreeing with it. difficulty_rises_with_band holds in 52.5% of grid worlds, so a rising curve is achievable — but not from altitude alone at these band spacings.
  • minor mass is the only working difficulty lever (§4.5, design) — It does get harder, and cargo mass is doing essentially all of it. Holding band fixed, empty → full hold moves peak heat 100 → 150 and cheapest plate cost 20.6 → 47.1% (suborbital), 103 → 154 and 22.3 → 50.4% (low), 105 → 152 and 23.6 → 49.8% (high): a 2.1–2.3x cost increase for a 2.21x mass ratio (606.8 kg of cargo on a 500 kg dry ship, 101.13 kg/slot). Altitude contributes 1.15x over the same span. Risk 2 is cleared on the evidence, but by mass and by the band-gated ablation tolls (2.0 / 2.3 / 2.9%), not by the altitude mechanism §2.3.7 credits.
  • minor unstaged braking survives on a perfect attitude (§2.3.1, tuning) — They are: the shallow unstaged pass peaks 80 (suborbital) and 72 (low) on the 100 bar, survivable = true in both shipping bands, leaving 20 and 28 points of headroom before cargo damage. In the grid unstaged_pass_survivable holds in 74.1% of worlds. The margin is measured with off_retrograde_penalty 1 and a perfect retrograde hold; the simulator models no attitude error, so a real pilot's drift comes straight out of those 20 points.
  • minor ascent leaves fuel undecided (§2.3.6, tuning) — All three bands were reached with 55.6 / 51.7 / 46.7% of the tank remaining (166.82 / 155.01 / 139.99 kg of 300). Even the high band, which is not shipping this semester, leaves nearly half a tank. fuel_margin_sane holds in 66.7% of grid worlds, so the target passes, but at 3.4 s of remaining burn per band nothing in the flown ascents makes fuel scarce, and the fuel_tank upgrade line (300 → 415 → 560) is buying capacity the flight profile does not use.

What the simulator cannot tell you
  • The exploration grid's soft landings do not reconcile with the published canopy. Top configs land a full hold at 3.00–4.86 m/s at sea-level densities as low as 0.02 kg/m3, where a fixed 700 m2 chute at Cd 1.5 would give a terminal velocity near 45 m/s. The grid must be solving canopy area per world, or bypassing the deploy gate the verification run enforces. Trust the direction of that result — lower gravity and lower entry speed make a soft landing reachable — and not the magnitudes; this is the one place I think the simulator, not the config, is the thing to fix first.
  • The descent profile flown is not the profile GDD 2.3.1 describes. The sweep flies fully staged from a circular orbit at one fixed braking periapsis, so the last pass is always a decayed-orbit entry rather than a shallower version of the first, and there is no phase where unstaged coarse passes walk apoapsis down while thrust remains. That structurally biases the measured optimum toward one pass and structurally prevents a slow final approach. The ablation finding is robust regardless (the arithmetic is monotone), but the size of the gap is a property of this profile.
  • Nothing in either sweep prices cargo damage. Plate percentage is the only cost in cheapest_ablation_pct, so the 6 of 9 descents that peak above the 100 heat cap are scored as if the haul arrived intact. Adding the cargo cost GDD 2.3.1 specifies would change which descents are 'cheapest' in currency terms, though not the pass-count ordering, since multi-pass descents spend more total time above the cap, not less.
  • The grid never varied planet radius, band altitudes, or the heating and plasma thresholds. If those thresholds are ratio-scaled per world (as baseline.json derives them), then lowering gravity moves the chute gate down with the orbital speed and buys nothing on the gate — only the terminal-velocity effect survives. My gravity proposal is ordered fifth for exactly that reason; the drag-coefficient move does not depend on which way that goes.
  • optimum_in_window is satisfied by roughly ten of 324 worlds and none of them appear in the top list, so I cannot inspect what those ten did. 'Essentially unreachable' is a strong read of 3.1%, not a proof of zero — if someone wants the U-curve badly enough, those ten configs are the only evidence that it exists anywhere, and they are worth dumping before the fallback is adopted.
  • No EVA was flown. The sweep covers ascent, descent and touchdown only, so every §2.3.4 claim — per-tether shear, no shear under steady tow, latch and swing jerk, tether slot economics, the 45 kg suit against a 403 kg tethered medium — is unverified by measurement. The economy-balancer's 5.0x steady-state margin is arithmetic, not flight data, and the suit-mass defect it flags is real but outside anything I measured.
  • The simulator is a point mass with a perfect pilot: no attitude error, no debris collisions, no control input. Every heat figure, including the 80/72 unstaged peaks and the 20-point margin they leave, is a best case that real off-retrograde drift eats into, and off_retrograde_penalty 1 means the config currently prices that drift at zero.

Spec audit — FAIL

Fourteen of sixteen checks pass and the catalog is internally clean, but the two rules that decide how a run ends both fail in flight: the cheapest descent is a single plunge in all nine measured descents despite a published cost curve whose minimum is 3, and no descent lands soft — touchdown is 21–23 m/s because the parachute never opens, which makes the 4.59 m/s full-hold figure and the whole of GDD 2.3.2 inert as flown.

ablation_optimum_two_to_four_passes — GDD §2.3.1
The two ablation knobs are tuned so the cheapest descent is 2-4 committed passes from every band — never a single plunge and never feathering.
Found: The published curve is internally sound. Recomputing cost(n) = n*toll + 0.00074*n*(heat_index/n)^2.2: suborbital n=1 = 2.0 + 0.00074*25118.9 = 20.59 (array 20.59), n=3 = 6.0 + 0.00222*2240.4 = 10.97 (array 10.97); low n=2 = 4.6 + 0.00148*6661.5 = 14.46 (array 14.46), n=3 = 6.9 + 0.00222*2730.1 = 12.96 (array 12.96); high n=1 = 2.9 + 0.00074*42645 = 34.46 (array 34.46), n=3 = 8.7 + 0.00222*3803.6 = 17.14 (array 17.14). All six recomputations match to the stated digit, all three minima sit at n=3, and each matches optimal_pass_count. The rule still fails, because the flight does not run the array: measured cheapest_pass_count is 1 in 9 of 9 descents and plate cost is monotone increasing in n everywhere — suborbital empty 20.6 / 25.9 / 28.3 / 56.5% at 1 / 2 / 3 / 16 passes, low full hold 50.4 -> 70.7% across 1-6, high full hold 49.8 -> 91.7% across 1-9. The mechanism is measured, not inferred: the one-pass plunge peaks 100 while the shallowest two-pass descent peaks 106, so within a 90 m atmosphere carrying a 5 s heat time constant a shallow pass is hotter than a steep one. With cost = toll + C*peak^E, every alternative to n=1 adds a positive toll to a strictly hotter final pass, so no C > 0, E >= 1 or toll > 0 can invert the ordering. The curve claims 2-4 and the game delivers 1.
parachute_full_hold_soft_landing — GDD §2.3.2
Parachute descent speed at full hold is under the 5 m/s soft-landing threshold and above about 3.5 m/s, so the Parachute upgrade is a real purchase.
Found: The stated terminal velocity checks out and is now independently computable, which is an improvement: full hold 500+606.8 = 1106.8 kg gives sqrt(2*1106.8*30/(3.0*1.5*700)) = sqrt(21.082) = 4.59 m/s, under 5 with 0.41 to spare and above 3.5; empty gives sqrt(30000/3150) = 3.09 m/s, and 4.59/3.09 = 1.488 = sqrt(1106.8/500), so the square-root-of-mass rule holds on its own. Parachute tier 1 (Cd 1.5 -> 2.1) takes full hold to 3.88 m/s, a real purchase. But the flight never reaches terminal velocity: measured touchdown is 21.03-23.34 m/s vertical in all 9 descents, soft_landing is false in all 9, and the parachute deployed in none of them, because the ship still carries most of its orbital horizontal velocity at contact so total speed never falls under the 58 m/s plasma gate that arms the chute. As flown, 4.59 m/s is a number no landing produces, and every rule downstream of it — the 5 m/s grade line, no_gear_multiplier 2, fragile_multiplier 2, and the entire Cd 1.5/2.1/2.9 upgrade line — never fires. The win condition (set Armstrong's satellite down gently) rests on this.
Observations — pass the spec, worth a human's eye
  • The heat bar is calibrated so that the easiest possible descent — empty ship, suborbital, one pass — peaks at exactly 100, which is also reentry.heat_capacity. That makes the cargo-damage line equal to the best case: measured peak heat exceeds 100 in 6 of 9 descents (128 and 150 suborbital half/full, 133 and 154 low, 133 and 152 high, reaching 158 on deep multi-pass entries), so every loaded reentry in the shipping slice is over the line the design says destroys a cargo slot every 3 seconds. The sweep prices shield plate only and never charges that damage, which is why all nine still report landed = true.
  • Neither heat_shield tier touches heat_capacity — both move plate_capacity_pct only (100 -> 150 -> 210). GDD 2.3.1 says heat shield upgrades buy margin; as parameterised they buy passes, not peak. A player who keeps overheating cannot spend their way to a cooler reentry, only to more of them.
  • suit_mass_kg 45 against the catalog's post-rescale masses makes the astronaut a parked car rather than the tugboat 2.3.4 asks for. One low-band medium on the tether drops acceleration from 180/45 = 4.00 m/s2 to 180/448 = 0.402 m/s2, 10% of unloaded, and the heaviest legal pair drops it to 0.2235 m/s2, 5.6%. The greed decision collapses at the first piece, because the second one barely changes an already-immovable load. The Debris Designer declared this consequence and the Balancer accepted it as theirs to fix; it is still unfixed.
  • The band difficulty curve is carried entirely by cargo mass, not altitude. Empty single-pass peak heat is 100 / 103 / 105 across the three bands (a 5% spread) and cheapest plate cost 20.6 / 22.3 / 23.6% (15%), against ablation.heat_index's claimed 27% — because entry speeds are trapped between circular and escape speed at 151-164 m/s. Holding band fixed, empty -> full hold moves cost 20.6 -> 47.1%, 22.3 -> 50.4%, 23.6 -> 49.8%. GDD 2.3.7's 'each altitude band up is disproportionately hotter to return from' is not true as flown; risk 2 is cleared by mass and by the band-gated tolls instead.
  • Fuel is not a decision in the slice. All three bands were reached with 55.6 / 51.7 / 46.7% of the tank left (167 / 155 / 140 kg of 300), roughly 5 s of remaining burn, so the fuel_tank line at 400 and 1300 buys capacity the flight profile does not consume — and 'run out of fuel in space' is one of the two loss conditions in section 1.
  • The fragile premium is 3x per piece but only about 1.5x per slot, because a fragile piece pays uncrushed cost forever: a low-band fragile small is 468 across 2 slots (234/slot) against a solid small at 156 across 1. With a 6-slot hold and double damage on heat and landing, an optimising player may reasonably decide to ignore the glint — which 2.3.7 names as the failure mode that wastes the mechanic.
  • hand_magnet's two tiers move different stats — tier 1 buys range, tier 2 buys hold force. A player who wants a stronger magnet has to buy 380 of range they may not want first, and the 1250 tier-2 hold-force purchase is aimed at a shear mode that steady towing never reaches (5.0x margin at the heaviest legal load), so its value depends entirely on how often jerk shear actually fires in the hands.
  • Cross-checking the two blocking failures against each other: with touchdown at 21-23 m/s in every descent, landing.damage_per_ms_over 6 would charge roughly 100 points of damage on a rule intended to price a metre-per-second of overspeed. Whatever fixes the chute gate should be flown before that coefficient is trusted, since it has never been exercised in its intended range.
Catalog concerns raised by the Economy Balancer

Not audit findings. These are problems the Balancer hit while pricing the catalog and could not fix, because it does not own that data. On a failing run they are routed to the Debris Designer; on a passing run they land here.

  • The catalog's revision_notes size the rescale against 82.5 kg per slot, but that figure counts each piece once. Weighted by spawn_weight - which is how a player actually meets the population, and how the sweep computes a hold - the shipping slice is 101.13 kg per slot (suborbital 67.9, low 126.3). A 6-slot hold is therefore 606.8 kg, not the ~495 kg the notes predict, which is why full hold comes out at 2.21x dry mass instead of the 1.99x the Designer was aiming for. Nothing here is wrong enough to block the audit, but the doubling rule is being satisfied at the top of its tolerance rather than in the middle of it, and the arithmetic the catalog reasons with should probably be the spawn-weighted one.
  • satellite_bus_frame is the single largest driver of that gap: 403 kg at 2 crushed slots is 202 kg per slot carried at spawn_weight 9, the heaviest per-slot piece in either shipping band and roughly 2.5x the 82.5 kg/slot the rescale targeted. reaction_wheel_housing (312 kg, weight 11) and thruster_quad_cluster (357.5 kg, weight 8) compound it. If the Designer wants full hold nearer 2.0x without touching the ship, trimming the low band's three heavy non-fragile mediums is a smaller and more legible change than another uniform factor - and unlike a uniform factor it would not force the whole catalog to be re-priced again.
  • Not a defect, recorded because it constrains me and the Coder rather than the Designer: at these masses one tethered low-band medium (403 kg) against a 45 kg suit makes the astronaut effectively immovable, so suit mass and jetpack thrust have to rise together with the rescale. The Designer already declared this consequence; I am confirming from the pricing side that it is real and that it is on my side of the fence to fix, not theirs.
