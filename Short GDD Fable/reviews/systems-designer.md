# Systems Designer — Round 1

Scope of this review: core loop, progression, pacing, difficulty curve, economy math. Sections cited by number from `gdd.txt`.

## Finding 1 — Economic death spiral: bankruptcy is possible but undesigned
**Severity: MAJOR**

The economy as written can strand a player financially, and the document does not acknowledge it. Section 2.5: "Every launch costs money: Armstrong fills the tank, and repairs the hull/heat shield at a flat fee per %." Section 2.2: at 100% heat, "cargo damages one slot per ~3 s... hull only after all cargo." So a botched reentry destroys the cargo (income) AND damages the hull (a per-% repair bill), then the next launch still costs 2–3 cheap pieces' worth of fuel. Two bad runs in a row and cash can go below the cost of the next launch. The stated loss conditions (Section 1) are "stranded with no fuel, or 0 HP" — economic stranding on the ground is neither a designed loss state nor prevented by any floor (no debt mechanic, no free minimal refuel, no scrap-the-ship-for-parts). The Playtester's economy sweep (Section 3) asks whether a lazy run breaks even, but not whether a failed run can make the next run unaffordable. Either declare bankruptcy a real loss condition and design toward it, or specify the safety net.

## Finding 2 — No prices, no run count: playthrough pacing is entirely unspecified
**Severity: MAJOR**

Section 2.5 defines 9 upgrade purchases and Section 1 says the win object is "reachable and landable only near full upgrades" — so upgrade prices ARE the pacing of the entire game. Yet the document contains not one price, not one junk value beyond the relative "launch cost ≈ 2–3 cheap pieces," and no target for how many runs a playthrough should take (10? 40?). The economy sweep (Section 3) is described as answering "does a lazy run break even" and "is the satellite run beatable," but a sweep can only validate a curve against a target, and no target curve exists anywhere in the document. This isn't a tuning detail to defer — with a value-scales-with-mass gradient (2.6) and bigger holds (2.5), income likely grows superlinearly across the game, so late upgrades must cost dramatically more than early ones or the back half collapses to two runs. The document should state, at minimum: intended total run count, intended earnings of an average mid-game run, and a rough price ratio between tier 1 and tier 3.

## Finding 3 — The "land safer" purchase doesn't exist: difficulty ratchets one way
**Severity: MAJOR**

Section 2.1's shop row says the player's decision is "reach higher, land safer, or haul more." Section 2.5 then lists the actual upgrade tracks: Fuel tank (reach), Thruster (reach), Storage (haul) — and explicitly fixes the mitigation systems: "Parachute, heat shield, and jetpack ship at fixed mid-tier values." There is no "land safer" purchase. This is worse than a table typo, because the difficulty loop the document itself describes (Section 3: cargo mass → heat → later chute → harder touchdown) means every Storage purchase makes landings strictly harder, and the player has no systemic counter-investment — chute and shield never improve. Difficulty scales with progression with no purchasable relief valve, so the curve is monotonic and rests entirely on player skill growth. Either restore a landing-mitigation track or delete the "land safer" promise and accept (and state) that landing difficulty only rises.

## Finding 4 — The endgame object's rules are underspecified where they matter most
**Severity: MAJOR**

Section 2.6: Armstrong's satellite "roughly doubles the ship's mass, barely fits a maxed hold, and arrives damaged unless the touchdown is soft." Three unresolved questions sit inside that sentence. (a) What does "arrives damaged" mean for the win condition — do you win with a damaged satellite, win with reduced payout, or must you retry? The win condition in Section 1 just says "retrieve" it. (b) The heat-damage rule is written per-slot ("cargo damages one slot per ~3 s"); the satellite is a single object that "barely fits a maxed hold" — does it occupy one slot, all slots, and does one 3-second tick destroy the win object? (c) Chute descent speed scales with the square root of mass (2.3) and soft is under ~5 m/s; with mass doubled, is a soft landing achievable at fixed mid-tier chute values at all? The Playtester question "Is the satellite run beatable at full upgrades?" (Section 3) covers (c) empirically, but (a) and (b) are design decisions, not tuning outputs, and no sweep can answer them.

## Finding 5 — Jetpack fuel is a stated cost with no defined economy
**Severity: MINOR**

Section 2.4 makes jetpack fuel the price of the core greed decision ("every trip out costs jetpack fuel"), and Section 2.5 fixes the jetpack at mid-tier, but nothing defines the fuel loop: does EVA fuel draw from the ship tank or a separate suit tank, does it refill at the ship for free, and is it included in "Armstrong fills the tank"? Related: Section 1's loss condition "stranded with no fuel" doesn't say which fuel — if a player burns the jetpack dry hauling a heavy piece away from the ship, is that a loss state, a drift-back-forever, or an undefined softlock? For a game whose EVA loop is the middle third of every run, the resource that gates it needs its rules written down.

---

**Summary judgment:** The physics loop (mass → heat → chute window → touchdown) genuinely interlocks and is the document's strongest system work. The economy that is supposed to pace a full playthrough does not interlock with anything yet — it has no numbers, no floor, and one of its three advertised purchase axes doesn't exist.

## Round 2 — Cross-examination

### CONFLICTS

**vs. Adversarial QA, Finding 4 (cargo as ablative armor / mass-shedding self-correction).** QA's sharpest observation cuts directly at my Finding 3. I argued the difficulty curve is monotonic — every Storage purchase makes landings strictly harder with no counter-investment. QA points out that if destroyed cargo sheds mass mid-reentry, an overloaded ship cools itself and pulls the chute window earlier, so the punishment "softens exactly when it should bite." If that reading holds, my "no relief valve" claim is overstated: the system has an emergent relief valve. I argue my side: burning cargo is not a relief valve, it is a punishment *converter* — it trades the landing-difficulty penalty for the income penalty, and income is the resource that paces the whole game (my Finding 2). A player who survives by shedding cargo lands safely and broke, which feeds my Finding 1's death spiral rather than relieving anything. So my Finding 3 stands as written: there is still no *purchasable* mitigation, which is what the "land safer" shop text promises. But QA is right that until the destroyed-mass rule is specified, neither of us can actually state what the difficulty curve does at 100% heat — which makes their Finding 4 a prerequisite for tuning mine.

**vs. Player Psychologist, Finding 1 (onboarding, BLOCKING).** I'll push on the severity from the systems side. The document's own physics imply a natural early-game ramp the Psychologist's finding doesn't credit: heat scales with speed and mass (2.2, Section 3's stated chain), and a starter ship — tier-1 tank, tier-1 thruster, small hold, low altitude band — flies slow, light, and low. First reentries should be the *coolest* the game ever produces, and the "cargo before hull" ordering is itself a graduated-punishment design: it taxes income before survival. So the systems, as written, do scaffold the first hour even though no tutorial does. That said, the scaffold is accidental and unverified — nothing in the document states that a tier-1 reentry is survivably cool, and no Playtester question checks it (the sweeps test full-hold and satellite runs, not the starter ship). I'd argue BLOCKING is the wrong grade for a hazard the difficulty curve may already solve; MAJOR-pending-verification is defensible, and "add a starter-ship heat question to the reentry sweep" is the cheap fix that settles it.

**vs. Player Psychologist, Finding 5 severity (flat mid-game, MINOR) — and partially vs. Business Analyst, Finding 2.** The Psychologist rates the nine-beat meta-loop MINOR; I rated the same territory (my Finding 2) MAJOR, and I'll defend the gap: break-even lazy runs don't just feel flat, they produce *mathematically zero* progress toward a win condition that requires near-full upgrades — the document permits a stable orbit of playing-without-progressing, and without prices nobody can say how wide that dead zone is. That's a pacing defect, not a garnish. Conversely, I'd temper the Business Analyst's claim that "the only real player choice is ordering" and Fuel/Thruster are "pure gates": under a budget, purchase *order* is the economic decision loop, and Storage vs. reach ordering changes income rate per run, so ordering can be a real decision — but only if prices force trade-offs, and no prices exist. We agree on the disease (my Finding 2, their Finding 4) while disagreeing on whether the shop is structurally hollow or just unpriced.

### CONNECTIONS

**Bankruptcy is a three-lens convergence with a fictional fix sitting next to it.** My Finding 1, QA's Finding 5, and the Psychologist's Finding 3 independently found the broke-but-alive state; QA's framing is the sharpest — the state matches *neither* listed loss condition, so it's an undefined terminal state reachable through normal play. Combine that with the Narrative Critic's Finding 4 (Armstrong is sole buyer AND sole supplier — an unclaimed company-store theme) and the fix designs itself: Armstrong extends credit. A debt mechanic simultaneously closes the economy floor (my lane) and delivers the blue-collar exploitation theme (theirs). That's an interlock the document could get almost for free.

**The satellite endgame ambiguity is worse than any one review shows.** My Finding 4(b) (does one 3-second heat tick destroy a single-object win item?), QA's Finding 1 (destroyed satellite = permanently unwinnable, no covering fail state), the Narrative Critic's Finding 1 (the climax's outcome is a rules ambiguity), and the Psychologist's Finding 4 (frustration cliff) are the same hole viewed from four lenses. My math contribution: chute speed scales with √mass (2.3), so doubling mass raises descent speed ~41%; if the fixed mid-tier chute is tuned so an ordinary full-hold landing comes in near the 5 m/s soft threshold, the satellite run lands near 7 m/s and a soft touchdown is *impossible by construction*, not merely hard. No sweep can rescue that — it's a tuning corner the fixed-equipment decision (2.5) may have already painted shut.

**Both tuning paths fail without pacing targets.** The Feasibility Lead (Findings 1–2) and Business Analyst (Finding 3) show the sweep harness is under-budgeted and the hand-tuning fallback contradicts the "empirically determined" claim. Connect that to my Finding 2: a sweep can only validate against a target curve, and *hand-tuning needs targets even more* than a sweep does. So the document's tuning plan fails in both branches for the same missing input — intended run count, mid-game earnings, tier price ratios. Writing those three numbers down is upstream of the entire Section 3 methodology, whichever instrument survives.

### REVISIONS

- **Finding 1: upgrade MAJOR → BLOCKING.** QA's articulation convinced me — this is not a missing safety net on a designed loss state, it is an undefined terminal state that neither loss condition covers, reachable in the first hour by an average player. Three reviewers hit it independently from isolated contexts; that's the empirical signal that real players will too.
- **Finding 4: upgrade MAJOR → BLOCKING.** Sub-questions (a) and (b) combined with QA's destruction/persistence gap mean the win object can plausibly be destroyed with no defined outcome, and my √mass arithmetic suggests the soft landing may be unreachable at fixed chute values. An underspecified climax was MAJOR; a possibly-unwinnable one is BLOCKING.
- **Findings 2, 3, 5: hold as rated.** Finding 3 survives QA's ablative-armor challenge for the reasons argued above; Finding 5 stays MINOR in my lane, though I note QA's Finding 3 shows the same undefined jetpack rules also breach fail-state coverage, which is the stronger version of the point and properly theirs.
