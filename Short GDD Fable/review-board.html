The Disagreement

Conflicts from Round 2 the board could not settle. Both positions are stated as argued; the decision is escalated to the design owner.

A. Onboarding: how severe, and what fix is affordable?

player-psychologist

BLOCKING — first-session confusion-then-punishment is a rage-quit generator. The two quit-prone moments live in narrow numeric windows only a sweep can tune, so hand-tuning cannot rescue them.

business-analyst · feasibility-lead · systems-designer

Severity is unadjudicable without an audience statement — a grader playing 20 minutes is not a retail player. A tutorial is the first real feature creep in a disciplined document; the affordable fix is a constants-level rules change (free practice reentry, waived early repair fees), and tier-1 physics may already provide a natural ramp.

Escalated: state the audience, then choose tutorial-as-feature vs. zero-cost rules scaffolding.

B. "Land safer": restore the missing mitigation track, or delete the promise?

player-psychologist

Deleting the promise is unshippable — the endgame stacks the worst landing conditions on the longest run, and monotonic difficulty with zero purchasable relief leaves raw retry as the only lever.

feasibility-lead

The mitigation track is the one fix this schedule cannot buy — it adds a Coder session, QA invariants, shop UI, and a new dimension to both already-under-budgeted sweeps. Delete the promise from §2.1 and state that landing difficulty ratchets.

Escalated: honesty-and-cheap vs. shippable-experience-and-expensive. Systems-designer and business-analyst flagged the missing axis; both fixes remain on the table.

C. Sacrificial cargo: exploit to close, or mechanic to formalize?

adversarial-qa

Cheap-junk-as-ablative-armor inverts the greed penalty. Close it with deliberately punishing rules — highest-value slot burns first, destroyed mass does not shed. Two sentences.

player-psychologist

It is the design's only accidental player-agency mitigation; planned loss is motivating. Specify burn order deliberately, surface it in the UI, and make sacrificial padding an intentional mechanic.

Escalated: both sides agree the ambiguity must be resolved (the systems-designer notes the burn-order rule is a prerequisite for tuning either way); the direction is a design-intent call.

D. The finale's cruelty: soften it, or cash it?

player-psychologist

The satellite run risks a frustration cliff exactly where the player should feel mastery; repeatability needs relief.

narrative-critic

The terrifying finale is the correct dramatic apex of the #Feeling pillar. The fix is a graded ending — pristine vs. damaged producing different closing beats — not a softer landing. Fix the payoff before touching the difficulty.

Escalated: contingent on Issue #1 — both positions assume "damaged" gets a defined meaning first.

Full Board

Round 1 findings per reviewer, with Round 2 cross-examination beneath. Severity stamps show the post-cross-examination grade; upgrades are noted.

Lens: core loop, progression, pacing, difficulty curve, economy math.

BlockingEconomic death spiral: bankruptcy is possible but undesignedMAJOR → BLOCKING in R2

A botched reentry destroys the cargo (income) and damages the hull (a per-% repair bill); two bad runs in a row and cash can go below the cost of the next launch. Economic stranding on the ground is neither a designed loss state nor prevented by any floor — no debt mechanic, no free minimal refuel, no scrap-the-ship-for-parts.

MajorNo prices, no run count: playthrough pacing is entirely unspecified

Upgrade prices ARE the pacing of the entire game, yet the document contains not one price and no target for how many runs a playthrough should take. A sweep can only validate a curve against a target, and no target curve exists anywhere in the document.

MajorThe "land safer" purchase doesn't exist: difficulty ratchets one way

§2.1 advertises "reach higher, land safer, or haul more," but §2.5 fixes parachute, heat shield, and jetpack at mid-tier. Every Storage purchase makes landings strictly harder with no systemic counter-investment — the difficulty curve is monotonic and rests entirely on player skill growth.

BlockingThe endgame object's rules are underspecified where they matter mostMAJOR → BLOCKING in R2

Does a damaged satellite win? Does one 3-second heat tick destroy a single hold-filling object? Chute speed scales with √mass — with mass doubled, is a soft landing achievable at fixed mid-tier chute values at all? The first two are design decisions no sweep can answer.

MinorJetpack fuel is a stated cost with no defined economy

EVA fuel gates the middle third of every run, but nothing defines its capacity, refill rules, or whether burning it dry away from the ship is a loss state, a drift-back-forever, or an undefined softlock.

Lens: onboarding, confusion, frustration, churn risk.

BlockingNo onboarding path for a mechanic that punishes first attempts with permanent, irreversible loss

Reentry is the game's most complex and least forgiving system, and a new player's first two or three reentries will almost certainly destroy their haul while learning stage → plasma → chute → gear. Losing a session's reward to an untaught rule is a classic rage-quit generator, in the first 20 minutes of play.

MajorThe most punishing decision in the game hangs on a single subtle color cue

The only signal for chute safety is the hull glow fading white-to-orange — a low-contrast, single-channel cue gating a binary catastrophic outcome, unreadable for ~8% of male players, with no audio cue or HUD backup (the reentry sensor was cut).

BlockingFailure recovery is undefined; the economy can plausibly death-spiral into a quitMAJOR → BLOCKING in R2

The loss conditions never say what happens next — restart, reload, save system? A player who crashes twice pays full repairs with nothing sold; nothing addresses the broke-player state. A softlocked slow-motion game over.

MajorThe win condition stacks the game's three cruelest constraints on its longest run, with an ambiguous "damaged" outcome

The finale demands the game's most precise landing under the worst landing conditions at the end of the longest flight; every failed attempt costs the full round trip plus repairs. Beatable-once-by-a-sweep is not reasonably-repeatable-by-a-human.

MajorNine total purchases with break-even floors risks a flat motivation curve in the mid-gameMINOR → MAJOR in R2

Between upgrades a player has nothing to buy, no intermediate rewards, and break-even runs produce literally zero progress. The greed loop is genuinely good moment-to-moment psychology, but the meta-loop above it is nine beats long with silence in between.

Answering the sweep questions requires an automated pilot flying the entire mission loop — launch, EVA, staged reentry, chute timing, graded soft landing. That is a control problem in its own right, nowhere in the document; the half-day timebox covers the trivial harness, not the pilot.

The custom fixed-timestep integrator is the foundation for everything, including the determinism that enables the Playtester — yet there is no build order, no Day-1 gate, and no determinism test in the QA invariant list.

BlockingToken budget has zero rework margin and untracked work itemsMAJOR → BLOCKING in R2

The ~1.2M/week arithmetic assumes every Coder session lands in one pass, but the QA red-test loop guarantees return trips. ~11–12 Coder sessions consume the week with no allowance for shop UI, HUD, menus, art integration, or Opus's faster burn against Pro limits.

MinorQA "reads the spec, not the implementation" has no API contract

GUT tests must instantiate real classes and call real method names — none of which exist in the spec. Without a pre-agreed API contract, QA either blows its 25k budget or produces tests that fail to compile.

If the heaviest object in the game burns up or is destroyed on a hard landing, the player may be locked into a permanently unwinnable save with no listed fail state covering it.

Burn the jetpack dry while tethered to a heavy piece — stranded, dead, or drift-forever? Can the player EVA into plasma? Does the ship hold station while the player is out hauling? No rules for any of it.

If cheapest-first or player-orderable, optimal play pads the hold with worthless panels as sacrificial heat shielding — inverting the greed penalty. If destroyed mass sheds, an overloaded ship cools itself mid-reentry, softening the punishment exactly when it should bite.
